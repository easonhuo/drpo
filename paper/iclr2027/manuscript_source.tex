\begin{abstract}
Off-policy policy optimization reuses historical behavior, including
negative-advantage samples that suppress known failures. Repeated reuse can turn this useful signal into excessive repulsion: as the
learner moves away from a historical negative action, subsequent updates make
that action increasingly remote without necessarily reducing its update strength.
Aggregate theory characterizes the transition from stable displacement beyond
the positive-only target to persistent drift and loss of finite stable equilibria;
controlled strength sweeps show that intermediate displacement can improve
held-out reward. The relevant
learner-relative coordinate is squared standardized distance for Gaussian
policies and surprisal for categorical policies. Dynamic Remoteness-Aware Policy Optimization (DRPO) leaves the negative
update unchanged in the near field and exponentially attenuates its remote
tail.
DRPO restores eventual inward Gaussian drift for every fixed finite
negative-to-positive mass ratio, yields an explicit ultimate-bound radius, and
changes categorical support suppression from exponential to polynomial
probability decay. External diagnostics and controlled experiments identify
remoteness-dependent policy geometry as a source of negative-update
amplification and show that selective tapering preserves useful local
feedback. DRPO achieves higher aggregate scores than the reported matched
controls on D4RL locomotion and nine structured-generation tasks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Policy-based methods learn from signed feedback: positive updates pull the
policy toward actions estimated to be preferable, whereas negative updates
suppress those estimated to be undesirable. Negative feedback can encode useful boundaries and exclusion information
absent from positive-only learning \citep{zhu2025negative,song2025good}. It can displace the policy beyond the positive-only solution; whether that
displacement improves task utility remains an empirical question. In off-policy settings, a historical sample may be reused after earlier updates
change its current-policy probability; we study the zero-refresh limit for
controlled isolation. When does repeated reuse turn useful negative feedback into excessive
repulsion?

Learner-relative remoteness is the state variable linking successive reuse steps. A negative update lowers the probability of its historical action, increasing
its remoteness and thereby changing the next policy-score response (the \emph{near field}
\(D\leq\tau\) versus the \emph{far field} \(D>\tau\);
Section~\ref{sec:drpo}). Policy family determines the tail law: Gaussian mean scores grow with
standardized distance, whereas categorical logit scores remain bounded even
as selected-action probabilities approach zero. Under isolated reuse,
exponential tapering changes Gaussian remoteness growth from geometric to
logarithmic and categorical probability decay from exponential to polynomial. This is the curse of repulsion: a negative update makes the same historical action
more remote on reuse without making the signal self-limiting.

Whether remoteness itself amplifies negative updates independently of sample
quality remains unresolved. Existing methods control off-policy
instability through filtering, importance weighting, probability-aware gates,
and tapered updates
\citep{novati2019remember,fakoor2020p3o,peng2019advantage,
schulman2017proximal,fujimoto2019off,kumar2020conservative,
arnal2025asymmetric,leroux2025tapered}, but remoteness is usually confounded
with advantage magnitude and data quality. We match those factors, vary only remoteness, and intervene separately on
near- and far-field negative updates to test downstream effects.

Our aggregate analysis places these per-sample effects in competition with
positive attraction. When positive mass dominates, repulsion can displace the
policy beyond the positive-only target while preserving a finite stable
equilibrium. At balance, the restoring force disappears and persistent drift
emerges; under negative dominance, no finite stable equilibrium remains.
These regimes characterize policy geometry and stability; task utility remains
an empirical question.

We therefore introduce Dynamic Remoteness-Aware Policy Optimization
(DRPO), which recomputes current-policy remoteness, leaves near-field negative
updates unchanged and exponentially attenuates those beyond the threshold. The taper dominates every finite-order
far-field response, restores eventual inward Gaussian drift for any fixed
finite negative-to-positive mass ratio, and makes categorical suppression
self-limiting. Remoteness controls geometric exposure, not directional utility
(Section~\ref{subsec:distance_dependent_utility}).

We contribute: \textbf{(1)} a characterization of policy-relative remoteness
under repeated negative reuse and aggregate regimes for stable extrapolation,
persistent drift, and loss of finite stable equilibria; \textbf{(2)} a
control-order hierarchy and selective exponential taper with Gaussian
ultimate-boundedness and self-limiting categorical suppression guarantees; and
\textbf{(3)} controlled experiments that separate coefficient magnitude
from policy geometry, identify a far-field causal pathway, and test the
stability--utility trade-off, together with matched external diagnostics
and task-performance evaluations in D4RL locomotion and structured generation.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Off-policy historical reuse across policy domains.}
Off-policy policy optimization reuses historical data, from replay and
offline-RL datasets in continuous control to fixed preference and verifier-labeled
corpora in language-model post-training.
Controls for historical-data reuse include importance weighting, conservative
or behavior-regularized objectives, and reference-regularized off-policy
optimization
\citep{precup2000eligibility,fujimoto2019off,kumar2020conservative,
fujimoto2021minimalist,kostrikov2021offline,tang2026agro}. Truncated importance ratios also underlie corrected off-policy return
estimators such as Retrace and V-trace
\citep{munos2016safe,espeholt2018impala}, primarily for distribution correction
rather than learner-relative negative-tail control. Our narrower focus is repeated reuse of signed actor feedback, especially the
negative branch, after the learner moves away from the historical action.

\textbf{Controlling negative updates.}
Controls span advantage-weighted policy fitting, which downweights
low-advantage samples
\citep{peng2019advantage,nair2020awac,kostrikov2021offline}; fixed global
down-weighting of negative advantages, as in HPO's hysteretic weight
\citep{sana2026hpo}; baseline-based positive--negative rebalancing in
AsymRE \citep{arnal2025asymmetric}; reference-relative preference
optimization in DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct}; and probability-aware
tapering in TOPR \citep{leroux2025tapered}. Unlikelihood and preference-based training also exploit failed or rejected behavior
\citep{welleck2019neural,ethayarajh2024kto,duan2024negating,
zhang2024negative,zhu2025negative,song2025good}.
DRPO instead treats learner-relative remoteness as a reuse-induced dynamical
state and attenuates only the far-field negative tail.

\subsection{Off-Policy Signed Actor Updates}

We consider a historical update distribution $\nu$ over state--action pairs,
which may represent an offline dataset, replay buffer, or trajectories from
earlier or stale behavior policies. Let $\pi_\theta(a \mid s)$ denote the current policy and
$\widehat{A}(s,a)$ the actor's advantage-like signal. During an actor step, the update distribution and advantage estimates are
treated as fixed with respect to $\theta$. The empirical signed actor field has score-function form
\citep{williams1992simple,sutton2000policy}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}(\theta)
    =
    \mathbb{E}_{(s,a)\sim\nu}
    \left[
        \widehat{A}(s,a)
        \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
    \right],
    \label{eq:empirical_actor_field}
\end{equation}
with update
\(\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\eta\mathbf{F}(\theta_t)\).
Repeated reuse evaluates the same historical samples and signed feedback
under an evolving current policy; Section~\ref{sec:theory} studies the induced
actor-side dynamics.

Write
\(\widehat A^{+}=\max\{\widehat A,0\}\) and
\(\widehat A^{-}=\max\{-\widehat A,0\}\), giving the actor-field decomposition
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}(\theta)
    ={}
    \mathbb{E}_{\nu}
    \left[
        \widehat{A}^{+}(s,a)
        \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
    \right] 
    -
    \mathbb{E}_{\nu}
    \left[
        \widehat{A}^{-}(s,a)
        \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
    \right].
\label{eq:signed_actor_decomposition}
\end{equation}
For a negative sample, the update magnitude factorizes as
$\widehat{A}^{-}(s,a)\lVert\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)\rVert$,
separating disadvantage magnitude from learner-relative policy geometry.
We call \(\widehat A^-\) \emph{coefficient magnitude},
\(\|\nabla\log\pi\|\) \emph{score response}, their product \emph{update
magnitude}, the squared remoteness gradient \emph{reuse loop gain}
(Section~\ref{sec:distance_strength}), and an expectation or sum of signed
updates \emph{aggregate force}.

Under the common update base measure \(\nu\), let
\begin{equation}
    p=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^+],
    \qquad
    q=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-],
    \qquad
    \rho=q/p \quad (p>0).
    \label{eq:aggregate_masses}
\end{equation}
As population expectations under \(\nu\), \(p\) and \(q\) already include
positive and negative sample frequencies.

\section{Far-Field Dynamics of Historical Policy Updates}
\label{sec:theory}



\subsection{Learner-Relative Remoteness and Update Strength}
\label{sec:distance_strength}

To isolate action-side geometry, fix context $s$ and write $u$ for its
policy-output coordinates. For a historical action $a$, define learner-relative remoteness as
\(
    D_u(a)
    =
    -\log \pi_u(a).
\)
For categorical policies, $D_u(a)$ is selected-action surprisal; for Gaussian
policies, it is a scale-dependent constant plus half the squared Mahalanobis
distance from the policy mean. Thus, $D_u(a)$ measures how unlikely the
historical action is under the current learner, not an action-space metric.
Define the squared score norm by
\(
    R_u(a)
    =
    \left\|
        \nabla_u \log \pi_u(a)
    \right\|_2^2.
\)
A sample with advantage estimate $\widehat{A}(s,a)$ contributes output-space
update magnitude $|\widehat{A}(s,a)|\sqrt{R_u(a)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Distance-dependent score-function magnitude]
\label{prop:distance_strength}
Fix a context and a historical action \(a\).

For a Gaussian policy with mean \(\mu\) and fixed covariance
\(\Sigma\succ0\), let
\(C_\Sigma=\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi)+\frac12\log|\Sigma|\) denote the
Gaussian normalization constant, which is independent of \(a\) and
\(\mu\). Then learner-relative remoteness satisfies
\(
    D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma
    =
    \frac12
    (a-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu).
\)
Moreover, the squared mean-score norm
\(R_\mu(a)=\|\nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)\|_2^2\) is bounded above and below
by positive constants times \(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma\), with constants
determined only by \(\Sigma\). Consequently, as standardized distance
increases, the Gaussian mean-score response grows without bound. When
\(\Sigma=\sigma^2I\), \(R_\mu(a)=2(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma)/\sigma^2\), so
remoteness and squared mean-score norm have the same global ordering.

For a categorical policy with logits \(z\), learner-relative remoteness is
the selected-action surprisal \(D_z(a)=-\log\pi_z(a)\). The selected-logit
score response is
\(
    \left|
    \frac{\partial}{\partial z_a}
    \log \pi_z(a)
    \right|
    =
    1-e^{-D_z(a)}.
\)
Thus, categorical surprisal can diverge as the selected probability
approaches zero, but the selected-logit response saturates at one. The
full logit-score norm remains bounded as well.
\end{proposition}

(Proof: Appendix~\ref{app:distance_strength}.)


\subsection{Self-Amplification under Historical Reuse}
\label{sec:self_amplification}

We now fix a historical action and reuse it as the learner changes. Write
\(
    D(u)=D_u(a),
    \qquad
    R(u)=\|\nabla_uD(u)\|_2^2,
\)
and consider the repeated single-sample update
\begin{equation}
    u_{t+1}
    =
    u_t
    +
    \eta\widehat A\nabla_u\log\pi_{u_t}(a)
    =
    u_t
    -
    \eta\widehat A\nabla_uD(u_t).
    \label{eq:single_sample_reuse_update}
\end{equation}
Action \(a\) and signed coefficient \(\widehat A\) remain fixed over the
reuse window.

\begin{theorem}[Self-attenuation and self-amplification under reuse]
\label{thm:reuse_dynamics}
Assume that \(D(u)\) is differentiable and convex on a convex
neighborhood containing the update segments, and define
\(
    D_t=D(u_t),
    \qquad
    R_t=R(u_t).
\)
If \(\widehat A<0\), then every reuse step satisfies
\(
    D_{t+1}
    \ge
    D_t+\eta|\widehat A|R_t,
    \qquad
    R_{t+1}\ge R_t.
\)
Thus, negative reuse makes the historical action increasingly remote
while preventing its score-function norm from decreasing.

If \(\widehat A>0\), and \(D\) additionally has an \(L\)-Lipschitz
gradient on the same neighborhood with \(0<\eta\widehat A\le 1/L\), then
\(
    D_{t+1}
    \le
    D_t-\frac{\eta\widehat A}{2}R_t,
    \qquad
    R_{t+1}\le R_t.
\)
Thus, positive reuse makes the historical action increasingly compatible
with the learner while attenuating its subsequent score response.
\end{theorem}

In both policy parameterizations, the theorem's convexity assumption holds
globally: fixed-covariance Gaussian remoteness is quadratic in the mean, and
categorical negative log-likelihood is convex in logits
(Appendix~\ref{app:reuse_dynamics}).
Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:distance_strength}, the theorem
distinguishes one-time distant updates from reuse feedback, yielding unbounded
amplification for fixed-covariance Gaussian mean updates and bounded but
persistent amplification for categorical logits.
For an isolated isotropic-Gaussian negative sample, remoteness grows
geometrically under uncontrolled reuse; Appendix~\ref{app:gaussian_reuse_rate}
gives the exact rate.

\subsection{Aggregate Equilibria under Historical Reuse}
\label{sec:aggregate_equilibria}

The preceding analysis tracks one fixed historical action; aggregating
positive and negative historical samples, we ask whether competing attraction
and repulsion yield a finite stable equilibrium, persistent drift, or
instability.
A common fixed-context exponential-family representation covers Gaussian and
categorical policies; Appendix~\ref{app:aggregate_equilibrium} gives the
coordinates and regularity assumptions. Using \(p,q,\rho\) from Equation~\eqref{eq:aggregate_masses}, let
\(\mathbf m_+\) denote the positive-only mean-parameter target. When \(q>0\), let \(\mathbf m_-\)
denote the magnitude-weighted negative-sample moment (a repulsion moment,
not an attraction target).

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate attraction--repulsion regimes]
\label{thm:aggregate_equilibria}
Consider the continuous aggregate actor dynamics and the corresponding
discrete update under the regular minimal exponential-family
representation specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:aggregate_equilibrium}. Assume \(p>0\).

\textbf{Positive-only case \((\rho=0)\).}
If \(\mathbf m_+\) lies in the interior of the attainable mean-parameter
space, there is a unique finite equilibrium whose mean parameter is
\(\mathbf m_+\). It is locally asymptotically stable in continuous time
and for sufficiently small discrete step sizes.

\textbf{Positive-dominant regime \((0<\rho<1)\).}
Define the signed target
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf m^\star(\rho)
    =
    \mathbf m_+
    +
    \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}
    \left(
        \mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-
    \right).
    \label{eq:signed_target_ratio}
\end{equation}
If \(\mathbf m^\star(\rho)\) lies in the interior of the attainable
mean-parameter space, there is a unique finite equilibrium whose mean
parameter is \(\mathbf m^\star(\rho)\). It is locally asymptotically
stable in continuous time and for sufficiently small discrete step
sizes. Holding \(\mathbf m_+\) and \(\mathbf m_-\) fixed, its displacement
from \(\mathbf m_+\) grows without bound as \(\rho\to1^-\), unless
\(\mathbf m_+=\mathbf m_-\). If the signed target leaves the attainable
mean-parameter space, no finite equilibrium exists.

\textbf{Critical regime \((\rho=1)\).}
If \(\mathbf m_+\neq\mathbf m_-\), the policy-output coordinate exhibits
persistent drift and admits no finite equilibrium. If
\(\mathbf m_+=\mathbf m_-\), every point is stationary, but no point is
an isolated asymptotically stable equilibrium.

\textit{Negative-dominant regime (\(\rho>1\)).}
Every finite stationary point, if one exists, is unstable in continuous
time and under every discrete update with positive step size. Hence, this
regime admits no locally asymptotically stable finite equilibrium.
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:aggregate_equilibrium} gives the proof, actor field, and exact
discrete-time step-size condition. Equation~\eqref{eq:signed_target_ratio} shows that while positive feedback
dominates, negative feedback displaces the equilibrium from \(\mathbf m_+\)
along \(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-\), away from \(\mathbf m_-\).

\subsection{Policy-Family Manifestations of Negative Dominance}
\label{sec:policy_family_manifestations}

Under negative dominance, no finite stable equilibrium exists, while score
and support manifestations differ by policy family.

\begin{theorem}[Policy-family score behavior under negative dominance]
\label{thm:policy_family_manifestations}
Assume \(p<q\).

\textbf{Gaussian policy.}
Consider a Gaussian policy
\(\pi_\mu=\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)\) with fixed
\(\Sigma\succ0\). Its aggregate mean dynamics have a unique finite
stationary point, which is repelling. Unless initialized exactly at that
point, the distance from it diverges under both the continuous dynamics
and every discrete trajectory with fixed step size \(\alpha>0\).
Consequently, for every fixed historical action \(a\),
\(
    \left\|
        \nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)
    \right\|_2
    =
    \left\|
        \Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu)
    \right\|_2
    \longrightarrow
    \infty.
\)

\textbf{Categorical policy.}
Consider a categorical policy
\(\pi_z=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\) with at least two actions and the
zero-sum gauge \(\mathbf 1^\top z=0\). Any finite stationary point, when
one exists, is unique and repelling. Unless initialized exactly at that
point, the continuous dynamics and every discrete trajectory with fixed
step size \(\alpha>0\) eventually leave every compact subset of the
logit space. Along some subsequence, the corresponding probability
vector approaches the boundary of the simplex. Nevertheless, for every
action \(a\) and every finite \(z\),
\(
\|\nabla_z\log\pi_z(a)\|_2^2
=
\|e_a-\pi_z\|_2^2
\le 2(1-\pi_z(a))^2
\le 2.
\)
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:policy_family_manifestations} gives the proof; fixed
covariance suffices for the Gaussian result.

\subsection{Why Selective Tapering Works}
\label{sec:selective_tapering_bridge}



\begin{lemma}[Far-field radial balance]
\label{lem:far_field_radial_balance}
For a fixed-covariance Gaussian field with finite negative atoms,
\(
F(\mu)=p\Sigma^{-1}(m_+-\mu)
 +\sum_iq_i\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i),
q=\sum_iq_i,
\)
and \(\mu=m_++rv\), \(\|v\|_2=1\),
\(
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\langle v,F(\mu)\rangle}{r}
=(q-p)v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v.
\)
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Selective tapering versus global scaling]
\label{cor:selective_vs_global}
If each negative atom is multiplied by
\(\omega_i(D_i)\to0\), the limit in
Lemma~\ref{lem:far_field_radial_balance} becomes
\(-p v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v<0\). Thus, for every fixed finite
\(\rho=q/p\), without \(\rho\)-specific tuning, the far-field radial
component is eventually inward; the onset radius is not uniform in
\(\rho\) and depends on the tail rate of \(\omega\). If additionally
\(\omega(D)\sqrt D\to0\), the absolute tapered negative force vanishes.
In contrast, global scaling \(\omega\equiv\alpha\) is inward only when
\(\alpha q<p\). Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:far_field_bridge}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Restoration by reference regularization]
\label{prop:regularized_restoration}
The regularized linear field has a unique finite stable equilibrium when
\(p>q\) for every \(\gamma\ge0\), when \(p=q\) for every \(\gamma>0\),
and when \(p<q\) if and only if \(\gamma>q-p\). At \(p=q\), its
displacement is \(O(\gamma^{-1})\). Reference regularization restores
stability by tethering the policy; DRPO instead removes the far-field
source. The proof and the Euclidean-spring variant are in
Appendix~\ref{app:regularized_restoration}.
\end{proposition}

These conclusions assume frozen data, fixed advantages, and unbounded reuse;
data refresh and actor--critic coupling alter the field.

\section{Dynamic Remoteness-Aware Policy Optimization}
\label{sec:drpo}

The selective-tapering analysis motivates DRPO: it targets the remote
negative source without uniformly weakening the negative branch.

\subsection{Update and Policy-Family Instantiations}
\label{subsec:drpo_update}

DRPO uses the thresholded exponential taper
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)
    =
    \exp
    \left\{
        -\lambda
        \left[
            \frac{D-\tau}{c}
        \right]_{+}
    \right\},
    \label{eq:drpo_exp_weight}
\end{equation}
where \(\tau\) is the threshold, \(c>0\) the remoteness scale, and
\(\lambda>0\) the decay rate. The weight is exactly one for \(D\leq\tau\) and
decays smoothly beyond it.

Using \(D_\theta(s,a)=-\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\), define
\(\bar D_\theta(s,a)=\operatorname{sg}[D_\theta(s,a)]\), where
\(\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]\) denotes stop-gradient. DRPO changes only the
negative branch:
\(
    \widetilde A_{\theta}(s,a)
    =
    \widehat A^{+}(s,a)
    -
    \omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}
    \!\left(\bar D_\theta(s,a)\right)
    \widehat A^{-}(s,a),
\)
with actor field
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf F_{\mathrm{DRPO}}(\theta)
    =
    \mathbb E_{(s,a)\sim\nu}
    \left[
        \widetilde A_{\theta}(s,a)
        \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)
    \right].
    \label{eq:drpo_actor_field}
\end{equation}
DRPO recomputes and detaches current-policy remoteness before weighting,
yielding a dynamic weighting rule rather than a differentiable distance
regularizer.

At each iterate, the aggregate field retains its form with negative mass and
moment replaced by
\(
q_\omega=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-\omega],
\qquad
\mathbf M_{-,\omega}=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-\omega T(a)].
\)
For \(0\le q_\omega<p\), the controlled signed target is
\(
\mathbf m_\omega^\star
=
\frac{p\mathbf m_+-\mathbf M_{-,\omega}}{p-q_\omega},
\)
with \(\mathbf M_{-,\omega}=0\) when \(q_\omega=0\).

\paragraph{Gaussian policies.}
For a Gaussian policy with mean \(\mu_\theta(s)\) and fixed covariance
\(\Sigma\), define
\(
    D_{\mathrm G,\theta}(s,a)
    =
    (a-\mu_\theta(s))^\top
    \Sigma^{-1}
    (a-\mu_\theta(s)).
\)
Because \(D_\theta-C_\Sigma=\frac12D_{\mathrm G,\theta}\), the constant
and factor \(1/2\) can be absorbed into the threshold and scale:
\(
    \omega_{\mathrm G}(s,a)
    =
    \exp
    \left\{
        -\lambda_{\mathrm G}
        \left[
            \frac{D_{\mathrm G,\theta}(s,a)-\tau_{\mathrm G}}
                 {c_{\mathrm G}}
        \right]_{+}
    \right\}.
\)

\paragraph{Categorical policies.}
For categorical policies, substituting selected-action surprisal into
Equation~\eqref{eq:drpo_exp_weight} gives
\(
    \omega_{\mathrm C}(s,a)
    =
    \min
    \left\{
        1,\,
        \left(e^{\tau}\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\right)^{\lambda/c}
    \right\}.
\)
\paragraph{Where existing off-policy controls intervene.}
We organize off-policy controls by four questions: what data are used, what
signal they carry, how much each sample contributes, and how policy is anchored
to historical behavior or a reference. These questions classify mechanisms, not algorithms; methods may instantiate
multiple mechanisms. DRPO acts at the sample-influence level, attenuating
negative feedback according to learner-relative remoteness rather than a
behavior-policy ratio. Appendix~\ref{app:topr_relation} gives the full
taxonomy and comparisons.

\subsection{Loop-Gain Order}
\label{subsec:min_order_control}

The next remoteness change is driven by the squared score
\(R(D)=\|\nabla D\|_2^2\), not by single-step parameter displacement; we call
\(R(D)\) the reuse \emph{loop gain}.

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian loop-gain order]
\label{prop:loop_gain_order}
For a fixed-covariance Gaussian mean policy,
\(I_{\mathrm{loop}}(D):=R(D)=\Theta(D)\) in the far field. Hence
\(\omega(D)I_{\mathrm{loop}}(D)\) is bounded exactly at the matching
order \(\omega(D)=O(D^{-1})\), and vanishes when
\(\omega(D)=o(D^{-1})\). Equivalently, the matching order is
\(O(r^{-2})\) in standardized distance \(r\), since
\(D-C_\Sigma=r^2/2\). The proof is in
Appendix~\ref{app:min_order_control}.
\end{proposition}

At the exact matching rate $\omega(D)=\Theta(D^{-1})$, weighted parameter
force vanishes as $\Theta(D^{-1/2})$ but weighted reuse loop gain remains
$\Theta(1)$, so an isolated reused atom can still drive remoteness outward at
constant order. For isolated Gaussian reuse,
this yields geometric growth without control, linear growth under
matching-order reciprocal tapering, and logarithmic growth under
DRPO's exponential taper
(Appendix~\ref{app:gaussian_reuse_rate}).

\paragraph{Utility boundary.}
\label{subsec:distance_dependent_utility}
Remoteness is a control coordinate rather than a utility label. When historical negative feedback no longer provides local correction, DRPO
targets $\omega(D)I(D)\to0$ as $D\to\infty$; task direction still comes from the
underlying advantage or verifier signal. Appendix~\ref{app:negative_update_utility}
gives the utility formulation.

\subsection{Guarantee Hierarchy}
\label{sec:exponential_taper}



\begin{proposition}[Vanishing finite-order far-field response]
\label{prop:exp_vanishing_influence}
Let \(I(D)\) denote the raw far-field response scale. If, for some
finite \(m\geq0\) and \(C>0\),
\(I(D)\leq C(1+D)^m\) in the far field, then the taper in
Equation~\eqref{eq:drpo_exp_weight} satisfies
\(
    \omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D) I(D)
    \longrightarrow 0
    \qquad
    \text{as }D\to\infty .
\)
Thus, the exponential taper eliminates any finite-order far-field tail
rather than merely bounding it.
\end{proposition}

The exponential is a convenient, non-unique envelope (proof:
Appendix~\ref{app:exp_vanishing_influence}).
The hierarchy strengthens from L0 (eventual inward Gaussian drift), through
L1 (vanishing far-field force) and L2 (bounded Gaussian loop gain), to L3
(vanishing finite-order tails). Global scaling fails L0, reciprocal-quadratic
is critical at L2, and exponential satisfies L0--L3; definitions and the
experimental-control mapping are in Appendix~\ref{app:exp_vanishing_influence}.
The hierarchy orders control guarantees.

With positive attraction and finitely many exponentially tapered negative
atoms, the fixed-covariance Gaussian mean field has ultimately bounded
trajectories and at least one finite equilibrium; if an uncontrolled stable
equilibrium lies inside the untapered region, the controlled and original
fields share the same local linearization there.
For a repeatedly suppressed categorical action, tapering changes far-tail
probability decay from exponential to polynomial without imposing a nonzero
probability floor. Appendices~\ref{app:gaussian_drpo_boundedness},
\ref{app:categorical_self_limiting}, and
\ref{app:categorical_restoring_balance} give full statements, branch conditions,
and proofs.

\subsection{From Useful Repulsion to DRPO Control}
\label{sec:repulsion_control}

Causal rescue does not imply removing all negative feedback. C-U1 and D-U1
place a hidden optimum beyond the positive-only target and direct local
repulsion toward it, while remote negatives add far-field pressure. This
separates useful repulsion from harmful dominance and enables controlled taper
comparisons.

We sweep effective negative-to-positive strength $q/p$ under matched-control
invariants, with Positive-only at $q/p=0$; Appendix~\ref{app:negative_strength_sweep}
gives protocol and metrics.
Figure~\ref{fig:phase_transition_6_4_1} shows a non-monotone transition:
small and moderate $q/p$ raise held-out-context reward above the positive-only
ceiling, showing useful repulsion beyond the positive-support solution; at
larger $q/p$, policy shift grows while reward falls, indicating
over-extrapolation. At the largest strengths, task-performance collapse and
boundary events appear; numerical failures remain separate.

Positive-only removes both the harmful tail and beneficial intermediate
regime; remoteness-aware control should preserve local repulsion while
suppressing the far-field tail.
Matched controls show that remoteness-aware tapering can preserve near-field
repulsion while suppressing far-field influence; global scaling reduces both. Appendix~\ref{app:controlled_taper_protocol} reports
Positive-only, Global-$\alpha$, Reciprocal-Linear, Reciprocal-Quadratic, and
exponential tapering under controls matched either by mean near-field weight
or by pre-Adam negative-gradient norm.

\section{Conclusion}

Repeated off-policy reuse can turn useful negative feedback into
self-reinforcing far-field repulsion, linking per-sample repulsion to stable
displacement, persistent drift, and instability. DRPO selectively attenuates remote negative updates while preserving local
ones; matched diagnostics and interventions support this mechanism across
continuous and discrete policies. Our guarantees analyze the zero-refresh limit for clean isolation, but the same
learner-relative weighting principle applies when negative samples are already
remote under the current policy, including replay and stale-policy settings;
remoteness-aware control is not restricted to static offline datasets.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:distance_strength}}
\label{app:distance_strength}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\delta=a-\mu\) and \(x=\Sigma^{-1/2}\delta\). For a fixed-covariance
Gaussian policy,
\[
D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma
=
\frac12\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad
\nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)
=
\Sigma^{-1}\delta,
\qquad
R_\mu(a)
=
x^\top\Sigma^{-1}x.
\]
The Rayleigh quotient therefore gives
\[
2\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})
\bigl(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma\bigr)
\le
R_\mu(a)
\le
2\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma^{-1})
\bigl(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma\bigr).
\]
Hence the Gaussian mean-score response is linearly comparable to
\(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma\) and grows without bound with standardized distance.
If \(\Sigma=\sigma^2I\), the bounds collapse to
\[
R_\mu(a)
=
\frac{2}{\sigma^2}
\bigl(D_\mu(a)-C_\Sigma\bigr).
\]

For a categorical policy, the \(K=1\) case is degenerate:
\(\pi_z(a)=1\), \(D_z(a)=0\), and the score is identically zero.
Assume \(K\ge2\) below and let
\(p_a=\pi_z(a)=e^{-D_z(a)}\). Since
\[
\nabla_z\log\pi_z(a)=e_a-\pi_z,
\]
the selected-logit response is
\[
\left|
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_a}\log\pi_z(a)
\right|
=
1-p_a
=
1-e^{-D_z(a)},
\]
which increases with surprisal and saturates at one. Moreover,
\[
R_z(a)
=
(1-p_a)^2+\sum_{j\ne a}\pi_z(j)^2.
\]
The non-selected probabilities are nonnegative and sum to \(1-p_a\), so
\[
\frac{(1-p_a)^2}{K-1}
\le
\sum_{j\ne a}\pi_z(j)^2
\le
(1-p_a)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{K}{K-1}(1-p_a)^2
\le
R_z(a)
\le
2(1-p_a)^2
\le
2,
\]
which proves the claimed bounded categorical score behavior.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:reuse_dynamics}}
\label{app:reuse_dynamics}

For fixed-covariance Gaussian means,
\[
D_\mu(a)
=
C_\Sigma+\frac12(a-\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu),
\qquad
\nabla_\mu^2D_\mu(a)=\Sigma^{-1}\succeq0.
\]
For categorical logits \(z\), with \(\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(z)\),
\[
D_z(a)
=
\log\sum_j e^{z_j}-z_a,
\qquad
\nabla_z^2D_z(a)
=
\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^\top\succeq0,
\]
because
\(v^\top(\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^\top)v
=\operatorname{Var}_{i\sim\pi}(v_i)\ge0\).
Thus both policy-output coordinates satisfy the theorem's convexity
assumption.

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
f=D,
\qquad
g_t=\nabla f(u_t),
\qquad
R_t=\|g_t\|_2^2.
\]

If \(\widehat A=-c<0\), let \(\alpha=\eta c\). Then
\(u_{t+1}=u_t+\alpha g_t\), and convexity gives
\[
D_{t+1}
\ge
D_t+g_t^\top(u_{t+1}-u_t)
=
D_t+\alpha R_t.
\]
Gradient monotonicity gives
\[
(g_{t+1}-g_t)^\top(u_{t+1}-u_t)\ge0.
\]
Since \(u_{t+1}-u_t=\alpha g_t\),
\(g_{t+1}^\top g_t\ge\|g_t\|_2^2\); Cauchy--Schwarz then yields
\(\|g_{t+1}\|_2\ge\|g_t\|_2\), with the zero-gradient case immediate.
Hence
\[
D_{t+1}
\ge
D_t+\eta|\widehat A|R_t,
\qquad
R_{t+1}\ge R_t.
\]
The second inequality is strict whenever the gradient changes
nontrivially along a direction of positive curvature.

If \(\widehat A=c>0\), again let \(\alpha=\eta c\), so
\(u_{t+1}=u_t-\alpha g_t\). The descent lemma gives
\[
D_{t+1}
\le
D_t-\alpha\left(1-\frac{\alpha L}{2}\right)R_t
\le
D_t-\frac{\alpha}{2}R_t
\]
whenever \(0<\alpha\le1/L\). For a convex \(L\)-smooth function,
cocoercivity gives
\[
(g_t-g_{t+1})^\top(u_t-u_{t+1})
\ge
\frac1L\|g_t-g_{t+1}\|_2^2.
\]
Let \(d=g_{t+1}-g_t\). Since \(u_t-u_{t+1}=\alpha g_t\),
\[
d^\top g_t
\le
-\frac{\|d\|_2^2}{\alpha L},
\]
and therefore
\[
R_{t+1}
=
\|g_t+d\|_2^2
\le
R_t+
\left(1-\frac{2}{\alpha L}\right)\|d\|_2^2
\le
R_t.
\]
Substituting \(\alpha=\eta\widehat A\) proves the positive-reuse claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Quantitative Gaussian Reuse Rates}
\label{app:gaussian_reuse_rate}

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative Gaussian reuse rates]
\label{prop:reuse_rate}
For an isotropic Gaussian mean policy with covariance \(\sigma^2I\),
one fixed negative sample of mass \(q>0\), and
\(X_t=D_t-C_\Sigma>0\), the uncontrolled update satisfies
\[
 X_{t+1}=\left(1+\eta q/\sigma^2\right)^2X_t,
\]
so remoteness grows geometrically. If the same sample is weighted by
\(\exp\{-a[D_t-\tau]_+\}\), \(a>0\), then its continuous-time
remoteness is \(O(a^{-1}\log t)\), and the discrete update obeys
\[
 X_t\le a^{-1}\{\log t+\log\log t+O(1)\}.
\]
This slowdown does not by itself prove aggregate boundedness; that requires
the closed-loop attraction established in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian_drpo_boundedness}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reuse_rate}]
Let \(\delta_t=\mu_t-a\) and
\(X_t=\|\delta_t\|_2^2/(2\sigma^2)=D_t-C_\Sigma\).
For an uncontrolled negative sample of mass \(q\),
\[
\delta_{t+1}
=\left(1+\frac{\eta q}{\sigma^2}\right)\delta_t,
\]
which gives the stated exact geometric recurrence.

Now let \(\omega_t=e^{-a[D_t-\tau]_+}\). Once
\(D_t>\tau\), write
\(b=\eta q/\sigma^2\) and
\(K_0=e^{a(\tau-C_\Sigma)}\). Then
\begin{equation}
X_{t+1}=X_t\left(1+bK_0e^{-aX_t}\right)^2.
\label{eq:app_tapered_gaussian_recurrence}
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\Delta X_t\ge0\), and for all sufficiently large
\(X_t\),
\begin{equation}
0\le\Delta X_t\le C X_te^{-aX_t}
\label{eq:app_gaussian_increment_bound}
\end{equation}
for a finite constant \(C\). Put \(Y_t=e^{aX_t}\). Since
\(aX_te^{-aX_t}\le1/e\),
Equation~\eqref{eq:app_gaussian_increment_bound} also gives a uniform
bound \(a\Delta X_t\le M\). Hence
\begin{align}
\Delta Y_t
&=Y_t(e^{a\Delta X_t}-1) \\
&\le C_1Y_t\,a\Delta X_t
\le C_2\log Y_t.
\label{eq:app_gaussian_y_increment}
\end{align}
The standard comparison sequence
\(\overline Y_t=K t\log t\), with \(K\) chosen larger than the
constant in Equation~\eqref{eq:app_gaussian_y_increment}, is eventually
a supersolution. Therefore
\(Y_t=O(t\log t)\) and
\[
X_t\le a^{-1}\{\log t+\log\log t+O(1)\}.
\]

In continuous time, the far-branch equation is
\(\dot X=C_3Xe^{-aX}\). Thus
\(Y=e^{aX}\) satisfies \(\dot Y=C_3\log Y\), and the same comparison
gives \(X(t)=O(a^{-1}\log t)\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Far-Field Radial Bridge}
\label{app:far_field_bridge}

\begin{proof}[Proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:far_field_radial_balance} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:selective_vs_global}]
Set \(\mu=m_++rv\) with \(\|v\|_2=1\). Direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
\frac{\langle v,F(\mu)\rangle}{r}
={}&-p\,v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v
+\sum_iq_i v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v \\
&+\frac1r\sum_iq_i
\langle v,\Sigma^{-1}(m_+-a_i)\rangle.
\end{align}
The final term vanishes, proving the lemma.

With atom-specific weights, \(D_\mu(a_i)\to\infty\) along every ray.
For finitely many atoms, \(\omega_i(D_i)\to0\) therefore makes both the
weighted coefficient of \(r\) and the weighted constant term vanish.
The radial limit is
\(-p v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v\), which is strictly negative. This is a
pointwise-in-\(q/p\) statement: the radius at which it becomes negative
can grow with \(q/p\). Moreover,
\(\|\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i)\|_2=O(\sqrt{D_i})\), so
\(\omega(D)\sqrt D\to0\) removes the absolute negative force. Finally,
for \(\omega_i\equiv\alpha\), the lemma applies with \(q\) replaced by
\(\alpha q\), yielding the condition \(\alpha q<p\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reference-Regularized Restoration}
\label{app:regularized_restoration}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regularized_restoration}]
The unweighted Gaussian field with a KL-geometry spring is
\[
F_\gamma(\mu)=\Sigma^{-1}
\{(q-p-\gamma)\mu+pm_+-qm_-+\gamma\mu_0\}.
\]
Whenever \(p-q+\gamma\ne0\), its unique equilibrium is
\[
\mu_\gamma^*
=\frac{pm_+-qm_-+\gamma\mu_0}{p-q+\gamma},
\]
and the field Jacobian is
\((q-p-\gamma)\Sigma^{-1}\). It is Hurwitz exactly when
\(\gamma>q-p\). This yields all three regimes in the proposition; for
\(p=q\), the displayed equilibrium has displacement proportional to
\(1/\gamma\). When \(p>q\), increasing \(\gamma\) contracts the
equilibrium toward \(\mu_0\), potentially sacrificing extrapolation.

For the Euclidean spring \(-\gamma(\mu-\mu_0)\), the Jacobian is
\((q-p)\Sigma^{-1}-\gamma I\). In the negative-dominant regime it is
Hurwitz exactly when
\(\gamma>(q-p)\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma^{-1})\); this condition is not
interchangeable with the KL-geometry coefficient above.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gaussian Closed-Loop Boundedness}
\label{app:gaussian_drpo_boundedness}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian DRPO boundedness]
\label{thm:gaussian_drpo_boundedness}
Let \(p>0\), \(\Sigma\succ0\), and
\[
F_\omega(\mu)=p\Sigma^{-1}(m_+-\mu)
+\sum_iq_i\omega_i(D_\mu(a_i))\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i).
\]
For finitely many negative atoms with exponential tapers, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_\omega&=\sup_\mu\left\|\sum_iq_i\omega_i(D_i)
\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i)\right\|_2,\\
R^*&=\frac{B_\omega}{p\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gaussian_bound_radius}
\end{equation}
Every continuous trajectory satisfies
\(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\mu(t)-m_+\|_2\le R^*\), and the field has at
least one finite equilibrium. If an uncontrolled stable equilibrium
places every negative atom strictly below its threshold, the controlled
and original fields have the same local linearization in a neighborhood of
that equilibrium.
\end{theorem}

The proof below gives an explicit
branch-wise bound, including the case where the shifted threshold is
negative and tapering is active from radius zero. Along every escaping
sequence,
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu)
=
p^{-1}\sum_i q_i\omega_i(D_i)
\longrightarrow 0;
\]
this explains how the remote negative source disappears, but it is a
diagnostic consequence rather than the boundedness argument.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian_drpo_boundedness}]
Let \(e=\mu-m_+\) and
\[
N_\omega(\mu)=\sum_iq_i\omega_i(D_i)
\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i).
\]
The finite-atom exponential-taper field is locally Lipschitz and has at
most linear growth, so its solutions exist for all forward times.
For \(V(e)=\frac12\|e\|_2^2\),
\begin{align}
\dot V
&=-p e^\top\Sigma^{-1}e+e^\top N_\omega(\mu) \\
&\le-p\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})\|e\|_2^2
+B_\omega\|e\|_2.
\label{eq:app_gaussian_lyapunov_bound}
\end{align}
Thus \(\dot V<0\) outside every ball of radius
\(R^*+\varepsilon\). The standard ultimate-boundedness argument gives
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\mu(t)-m_+\|_2\le R^*.
\]
On the boundary of any ball centered at \(m_+\) with radius greater
than \(R^*\), Equation~\eqref{eq:app_gaussian_lyapunov_bound} is
strictly negative. Continuity of the field and the Poincar\'e--Bohl
inward-pointing theorem therefore imply at least one zero inside the
ball. This is an existence result, not a uniqueness or global-convergence
claim.

It remains to verify \(B_\omega<\infty\) and the stated constant. Put
\(r_i=\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\mu-a_i)\|_2\), so
\(D_i=C_\Sigma+r_i^2/2\), and
\[
\|\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-a_i)\|_2
\le\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma^{-1})}\,r_i.
\]
For \(a=\lambda/c\) and
\(\widetilde\tau_i=\tau_i-C_\Sigma\), maximize
\[
g_i(r)=r\exp\{-a[r^2/2-\widetilde\tau_i]_+\},\qquad r\ge0.
\]
If \(\widetilde\tau_i<0\), the taper is active at zero and the maximum
occurs at \(r=a^{-1/2}\), giving
\(a^{-1/2}e^{a\widetilde\tau_i-1/2}\). If
\(\widetilde\tau_i\ge0\), the untapered plateau ends at
\(r_\tau=\sqrt{2\widetilde\tau_i}\). When
\(a^{-1/2}\le r_\tau\), the maximum is \(r_\tau\); otherwise it is the
same interior-tail value. These cases show that \(g_i\) has a finite
maximum, and the triangle inequality gives \(B_\omega<\infty\).

For near-field fidelity, let \(\mu^*\) be an uncontrolled equilibrium
with \(D_{\mu^*}(a_i)<\tau_i\) for every negative atom. Strictness and
continuity provide a neighborhood in which every \(\omega_i=1\).
The controlled and uncontrolled fields, including their Jacobians, are
therefore identical there.

Finally, along any sequence \(\|\mu_n\|_2\to\infty\), each fixed atom
satisfies \(D_{\mu_n}(a_i)\to\infty\), hence
\(\omega_i(D_{\mu_n}(a_i))\to0\). Finiteness of the atom set gives
\(\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu_n)\to0\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Derivation and Proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate_equilibria}}
\label{app:aggregate_equilibrium}

At a fixed context, use a regular minimal exponential-family
representation
\[
\pi_\xi(a)
=
h(a)\exp\{\xi^\top T(a)-\psi(\xi)\},
\qquad
\xi\in\mathcal H,
\]
and let
\[
\mathcal M
=
\{\nabla\psi(\xi):\xi\in\mathcal H\}
\]
be the attainable mean-parameter space. For a fixed-covariance Gaussian,
\(\xi=\Sigma^{-1}\mu\), \(T(a)=a\), and
\(\nabla\psi(\xi)=\mu\). For a categorical policy, a reference-logit
gauge gives the corresponding minimal \((K-1)\)-dimensional
representation. Regularity and minimality imply
\(\nabla^2\psi(\xi)\succ0\) on \(\mathcal H\) and make
\(\nabla\psi:\mathcal H\to\mathcal M\) one-to-one.

Write
\[
\widehat A=\widehat A^+-\widehat A^-,
\qquad
p=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^+],
\qquad
q=\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-],
\]
and define the unnormalized moments
\[
\bar{\mathbf m}_+
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^+T(a)],
\qquad
\bar{\mathbf m}_-
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-T(a)].
\]
Because \(p>0\), let
\(\mathbf m_+=\bar{\mathbf m}_+/p\); when \(q>0\), let
\(\mathbf m_-=\bar{\mathbf m}_-/q\). If \(q=0\), use only
\(\bar{\mathbf m}_-=\mathbf0\).

Set
\[
b
=
\bar{\mathbf m}_+-\bar{\mathbf m}_-.
\]
Up to a constant independent of \(\xi\), the signed objective, field,
and Hessian are
\[
\mathcal L(\xi)
=
b^\top\xi-(p-q)\psi(\xi)+C,
\qquad
\mathbf F(\xi)
=
b-(p-q)\nabla\psi(\xi),
\]
\[
J_{\mathbf F}(\xi)
=
\nabla^2\mathcal L(\xi)
=
-(p-q)\nabla^2\psi(\xi).
\]
We consider
\[
\dot\xi=\mathbf F(\xi),
\qquad
\xi_{t+1}=\xi_t+\alpha\mathbf F(\xi_t),
\qquad
\alpha>0.
\]

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Positive-dominant regime, including positive-only
\((p>q\ge0)\).}
Define
\[
\mathbf m^\star
=
\frac{b}{p-q}.
\]
A finite equilibrium is equivalent to
\[
\nabla\psi(\xi^\star)=\mathbf m^\star.
\]
Thus, if \(\mathbf m^\star\in\mathcal M\), existence and uniqueness
follow from the bijectivity of
\(\nabla\psi:\mathcal H\to\mathcal M\); if
\(\mathbf m^\star\notin\mathcal M\), no finite equilibrium exists.
Because \(p-q>0\),
\[
\nabla^2\mathcal L(\xi)
=
-(p-q)\nabla^2\psi(\xi)
\prec0,
\]
so any finite equilibrium is the unique finite maximizer of
\(\mathcal L\). Its continuous-time Jacobian is negative definite, hence
it is locally asymptotically stable. The discrete local Jacobian is
\[
J_{\mathrm{disc}}(\xi^\star)
=
I-\alpha(p-q)\nabla^2\psi(\xi^\star),
\]
whose eigenvalues lie strictly inside the unit disk whenever
\[
0
<
\alpha
<
\frac{2}{
(p-q)\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2\psi(\xi^\star))
}.
\]

When \(q=0\),
\(\bar{\mathbf m}_-=\mathbf0\) and
\(\mathbf m^\star=\mathbf m_+\), recovering the positive-only case.
When \(q>0\), with \(\rho=q/p\),
\[
\mathbf m^\star
=
\frac{p\mathbf m_+-q\mathbf m_-}{p-q}
=
\mathbf m_+
+
\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}
(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-),
\]
so
\[
\mathbf m^\star-\mathbf m_+
=
\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}
(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-).
\]
For fixed unequal \(\mathbf m_+\) and \(\mathbf m_-\), this algebraic
signed-target displacement grows without bound as \(\rho\to1^-\); a
finite equilibrium exists only while the target remains in
\(\mathcal M\).

If a sequence of reachable targets
\(\mathbf m_n^\star\in\mathcal M\) leaves every compact subset of
\(\mathcal M\), then the associated
\(\xi_n^\star=(\nabla\psi)^{-1}(\mathbf m_n^\star)\) leaves every compact
subset of \(\mathcal H\). Otherwise some compact
\(K\subset\mathcal H\) would contain a subsequence of
\(\xi_n^\star\), while its continuous image \(\nabla\psi(K)\) would be
compact in \(\mathcal M\) and contain the corresponding targets, a
contradiction.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Critical regime \((p=q>0)\).}
Here
\[
\mathbf F(\xi)
=
\bar{\mathbf m}_+-\bar{\mathbf m}_-
=
p(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-)
\]
is constant. If \(\mathbf m_+\ne\mathbf m_-\),
\[
\xi(t)
=
\xi(0)+tp(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-),
\qquad
\xi_t
=
\xi_0+t\alpha p(\mathbf m_+-\mathbf m_-),
\]
so both dynamics exhibit linear drift and have no finite equilibrium;
the drift persists for all forward time when \(\mathcal H\) is full or
forward invariant along this direction. If
\(\mathbf m_+=\mathbf m_-\), the field vanishes identically. Every point
is stationary, but no point is an isolated locally asymptotically stable
equilibrium.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Negative-dominant regime \((p<q)\).}
Now
\[
\nabla^2\mathcal L(\xi)
=
(q-p)\nabla^2\psi(\xi)
\succ0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal L\) is strictly convex. If a finite stationary point
\(\xi^\star\) exists, its continuous field Jacobian is positive
definite, so it is repelling. The discrete local Jacobian is
\[
I+\alpha(q-p)\nabla^2\psi(\xi^\star),
\]
whose eigenvalues are
\(1+\alpha(q-p)\lambda_i>1\) for every \(\alpha>0\).
Hence every finite stationary point is unstable in both time forms; if
no stationary point exists, there is no finite stable equilibrium.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:policy_family_manifestations}}
\label{app:policy_family_manifestations}

\begin{proof}
Let \(r=q-p>0\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Gaussian policy.}
Define the unnormalized signed moments
\[
\bar\mu_+
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^+(a)a],
\qquad
\bar\mu_-
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-(a)a].
\]
For a fixed-covariance Gaussian,
\[
\nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)
=
\Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu),
\]
so
\[
\mathbf F_\mu(\mu)
=
\Sigma^{-1}
\bigl[\bar\mu_+-\bar\mu_--(p-q)\mu\bigr].
\]
Define
\[
\mu^\dagger
=
\frac{\bar\mu_--\bar\mu_+}{r}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbf F_\mu(\mu)
=
r\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-\mu^\dagger).
\]
Writing \(\delta=\mu-\mu^\dagger\), the continuous and discrete
solutions are
\[
\delta(t)
=
e^{r\Sigma^{-1}t}\delta(0),
\qquad
\delta_t
=
\left(I+\alpha r\Sigma^{-1}\right)^t\delta_0.
\]
Since \(\Sigma^{-1}\succ0\),
\[
\|\delta(t)\|_2
\ge
e^{r\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})t}\|\delta(0)\|_2,
\]
and
\[
\|\delta_t\|_2
\ge
\left[
1+\alpha r\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})
\right]^t
\|\delta_0\|_2.
\]
Thus \(\mu^\dagger\) is repelling and every trajectory not initialized
exactly there diverges. For any fixed historical action \(a\),
\[
\|\nabla_\mu\log\pi_\mu(a)\|_2
=
\|\Sigma^{-1}(a-\mu)\|_2
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma^{-1})\|a-\mu\|_2
\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Categorical policy.}
Work on the zero-sum gauge
\[
\mathcal H_0
=
\{z\in\mathbb R^K:\mathbf1^\top z=0\}.
\]
Let the fixed signed action-mass vector be
\[
b
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A(a)e_a]
=
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^+(a)e_a]
-
\mathbb E_\nu[\widehat A^-(a)e_a].
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal L(z)
=
b^\top z
+
r\log\sum_{j=1}^K e^{z_j}
+
C,
\]
with gradient and Hessian
\[
g(z)
=
\nabla\mathcal L(z)
=
b+r\pi_z,
\qquad
H(z)
=
r\bigl(\operatorname{Diag}(\pi_z)-\pi_z\pi_z^\top\bigr).
\]
Here \(g\) is the aggregate objective gradient, not the single-action
score whose norm is bounded in Proposition~\ref{prop:distance_strength}.
Because
\(\mathbf1^\top b=p-q=-r\),
\(\mathbf1^\top g(z)=0\), so both continuous and discrete dynamics remain
in \(\mathcal H_0\). For every nonzero \(v\in\mathcal H_0\),
\[
v^\top H(z)v
=
r\,\operatorname{Var}_{A\sim\pi_z}(v_A)
>
0.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal L\) is strictly convex on \(\mathcal H_0\), has at most
one finite stationary point, and any such point is repelling: the
continuous Jacobian restricted to \(\mathcal H_0\) is positive
definite, while the discrete Jacobian restricted to \(\mathcal H_0\)
has eigenvalues strictly larger than one for every \(\alpha>0\).

It remains to prove escape for every nonstationary initialization. If
\(g(z_0)=0\), both the continuous and discrete dynamics remain at the
stationary point. Otherwise, since \(g(z)=b+r\pi_z\) is bounded, the
continuous flow \(\dot z=g(z)\) exists for all forward time, and
\[
c_0=\|g(z_0)\|_2^2>0.
\]
Along the flow,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|g(z(t))\|_2^2
=
2g(z(t))^\top H(z(t))g(z(t))
\ge0,
\]
so \(\|g(z(t))\|_2^2\ge c_0\), and therefore
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal L(z(t))
=
\|g(z(t))\|_2^2
\ge c_0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal L(z(t))\ge\mathcal L(z_0)+c_0t\).

For the discrete update
\[
z_{t+1}=z_t+\alpha g_t,
\qquad
g_t=g(z_t),
\]
gradient monotonicity gives
\[
(g_{t+1}-g_t)^\top(z_{t+1}-z_t)\ge0.
\]
Since \(z_{t+1}-z_t=\alpha g_t\),
\(g_{t+1}^\top g_t\ge\|g_t\|_2^2\), hence
\(\|g_{t+1}\|_2\ge\|g_t\|_2\ge\sqrt{c_0}\). Convexity then gives
\[
\mathcal L(z_{t+1})-\mathcal L(z_t)
\ge
g_t^\top(z_{t+1}-z_t)
=
\alpha\|g_t\|_2^2
\ge
\alpha c_0.
\]
Thus the discrete objective also grows at least linearly.

A continuous function is bounded above on every compact subset of
\(\mathcal H_0\). The preceding linear-growth bounds therefore imply
that every nonstationary continuous or discrete trajectory eventually
leaves every compact subset of \(\mathcal H_0\).

Finally, on \(\mathcal H_0\),
\[
z_i
=
\log\pi_z(i)
-
\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^K\log\pi_z(j).
\]
If the probability vectors stayed in a compact subset of the simplex
interior, all coordinates would be bounded below by some
\(\varepsilon>0\), and the inverse-softmax formula would keep the logits
bounded. Escape in \(\mathcal H_0\) therefore implies a subsequence along
which at least one action probability approaches zero. Nevertheless,
for every action \(a\),
\[
\|\nabla_z\log\pi_z(a)\|_2^2
=
(1-\pi_z(a))^2+\sum_{j\ne a}\pi_z(j)^2
\le
2(1-\pi_z(a))^2
\le2,
\]
as established in Proposition~\ref{prop:distance_strength}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Minimum-Order Control}
\label{app:min_order_control}

For fixed-covariance Gaussian mean coordinates,
Proposition~\ref{prop:distance_strength} gives
\(R(D)=\Theta(D-C_\Sigma)\). Since \(C_\Sigma\) is fixed,
\(D-C_\Sigma=\Theta(D)\) as \(D\to\infty\). Hence there are
constants \(0<c_1\le c_2<\infty\) and \(D_0\) such that, for all
\(D\ge D_0\),
\[
c_1D
\le
R(D)
\le
c_2D.
\]
For the nonnegative weight \(\omega(D)\),
\[
c_1D\,\omega(D)
\le
\omega(D)R(D)
\le
c_2D\,\omega(D).
\]
Thus \(\omega(D)R(D)\) is bounded if and only if
\(\omega(D)=O(D^{-1})\), and it vanishes if and only if
\(\omega(D)=o(D^{-1})\). Since
\(D-C_\Sigma=r^2/2\), the matching order is equivalently
\(O(r^{-2})\) in standardized distance. This statement concerns the
squared-score reuse loop gain \(R\); the parameter-force scale is
\(\sqrt R\), as distinguished in the main text.

\subsection{Empirical Negative-Update Utility}
\label{app:negative_update_utility}

Let \(\widehat J_{\mathcal B}(\theta)\) be an empirical task-performanceestimate on evaluation batch \(\mathcal B\). For a negative sample
\(z=(s,a)\), define
\[
g_\theta^-(z)
=
-\widehat A^-(z)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s),
\qquad
\Delta\theta(z;w)
=
\eta w g_\theta^-(z),
\qquad
0\le w\le1.
\]
Its empirical and population utilities are
\[
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{\mathcal B}(z;w\mid\theta)
=
\widehat J_{\mathcal B}\bigl(\theta+\Delta\theta(z;w)\bigr)
-
\widehat J_{\mathcal B}(\theta),
\]
\[
\mathcal U_\theta(z;w)
=
J\bigl(\theta+\eta w g_\theta^-(z)\bigr)-J(\theta).
\]
The empirical utility-optimal weight is
\[
\widehat w_{\mathcal U}^\star(z\mid\theta)
\in
\arg\max_{w\in[0,1]}
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{\mathcal B}(z;w\mid\theta).
\]
If \(\widehat J_{\mathcal B}\) is unbiased for \(J\), then for fixed
\(\theta,z,w\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal B}
\left[
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{\mathcal B}(z;w\mid\theta)
\right]
=
\mathcal U_\theta(z;w).
\]
Thus empirical utility is evaluation-batch dependent rather than an
intrinsic deterministic sample label.

\begin{lemma}[Far-field shrinkage of the utility-optimal step]
\label{lem:far_field_utility}
Let \(D\) index learner-relative remoteness and let \(g(D)\ne0\) be the
corresponding negative-update direction. Define
\[
I(D)=\|g(D)\|_2,
\qquad
v(D)=\frac{g(D)}{I(D)}.
\]
Suppose the local update space admits an
orthogonal decomposition
\[
\mathcal T
=
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{task}}
\oplus
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{far}},
\]
with projection \(P_{\mathrm{task}}\), and suppose that:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\nabla J(\theta)\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{task}}\);

    \item \(\|P_{\mathrm{task}}v(D)\|_2\to0\) as \(D\to\infty\);

    \item there exist \(\rho_0>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(D_0\) such that,
    for every \(D\ge D_0\) and every \(\rho\in[0,\rho_0]\),
    \[
    v(D)^\top
    \nabla^2J\bigl(\theta+\rho v(D)\bigr)
    v(D)
    \le
    -\kappa.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
Define
\[
\mathcal V_D(\rho)
=
J\bigl(\theta+\rho v(D)\bigr)-J(\theta),
\qquad
0\le\rho\le\rho_0.
\]
Continuity of \(J\) implies that \(\mathcal V_D\) attains a maximum
on the compact interval \([0,\rho_0]\); let
\[
\rho_{\mathcal U}^\star(D)
\in
\arg\max_{\rho\in[0,\rho_0]}
\mathcal V_D(\rho).
\]
Then
\[
\rho_{\mathcal U}^\star(D)\to0
\qquad
\text{as }D\to\infty.
\]
Moreover, for \(D\ge D_0\), if
\(\langle\nabla J(\theta),v(D)\rangle<0\), then
\[
\mathcal V_D(\rho)<0
\qquad
\text{for every }\rho\in(0,\rho_0].
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
a(D)
=
\langle\nabla J(\theta),v(D)\rangle.
\]
Because
\(\nabla J(\theta)\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{task}}\),
\[
|a(D)|
=
\left|
\left\langle
\nabla J(\theta),
P_{\mathrm{task}}v(D)
\right\rangle
\right|
\le
\|\nabla J(\theta)\|_2
\|P_{\mathrm{task}}v(D)\|_2
\longrightarrow0.
\]
For \(D\ge D_0\) and \(\rho\in(0,\rho_0]\), Taylor's theorem gives some
\(\xi\in(0,\rho)\), and the curvature assumption yields
\[
\mathcal V_D(\rho)
\le
\rho a(D)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\rho^2.
\]
The same bound is trivial at \(\rho=0\). Since
\(\mathcal V_D(0)=0\), a maximizing step must have nonnegative utility.
The right-hand side is strictly negative whenever
\[
\rho
>
\frac{2[a(D)]_+}{\kappa},
\]
so
\[
0
\le
\rho_{\mathcal U}^\star(D)
\le
\frac{2[a(D)]_+}{\kappa}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
If \(a(D)<0\), the same upper bound is strictly negative for every
\(\rho>0\), proving the second claim.
\end{proof}

This is a sufficient local mechanism, not a universal monotonicity law.
It applies when far-field negative updates become asymptotically
misaligned with the task-improving direction and the evaluated objective
is locally harmed along those directions. The curvature condition can
represent local losses caused by parameter drift or policy-support
contraction, provided those effects reduce the evaluated objective
\(J\). If far-field growth remains aligned with \(\nabla J(\theta)\),
the conclusion need not hold.

A sample weight \(w\) produces effective step length
\[
\rho(D;w)
=
\eta w I(D).
\]
Because the lemma is local to \([0,\rho_0]\), define
\[
w_{\mathcal U,\mathrm{loc}}^\star(D)
\in
\arg\max_{\substack{0\le w\le1\\ \eta wI(D)\le\rho_0}}
\mathcal V_D\bigl(\eta wI(D)\bigr).
\]
The same quadratic bound and feasibility of the zero step yield
\[
0
\le
\eta I(D)w_{\mathcal U,\mathrm{loc}}^\star(D)
\le
\frac{2[a(D)]_+}{\kappa}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
For fixed \(\eta>0\), if
\(\liminf_{D\to\infty}I(D)>0\), then
\(w_{\mathcal U,\mathrm{loc}}^\star(D)\to0\) as well.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:exp_vanishing_influence}}
\label{app:exp_vanishing_influence}

\begin{proof}
For \(D>\tau\),
\[
\omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)
=
\exp\left[-\frac{\lambda}{c}(D-\tau)\right].
\]
Under the finite-order response assumption,
\[
0
\le
\omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)I(D)
\le
C e^{(\lambda/c)\tau}
e^{-(\lambda/c)D}(1+D)^m
\longrightarrow0,
\]
because exponential decay dominates every finite-order polynomial.

When the response scale is instantiated by a raw negative-update
vector, let \(z_D\) denote a corresponding sample and write
\[
I(D)=\|g_\theta^-(z_D)\|_2.
\]
Define
\[
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)
:=
\eta\omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)g_\theta^-(z_D).
\]
Then
\[
\|\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)\|_2
=
\eta\omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)I(D)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
If \(\widehat J_{\mathcal B}\) is locally
\(L_{\mathcal B}\)-Lipschitz around \(\theta\), then for all sufficiently
large \(D\),
\[
\left|
\widehat{\mathcal U}_{\mathcal B}
\bigl(z_D;\omega_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)\mid\theta\bigr)
\right|
\le
L_{\mathcal B}
\|\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{Exp}}(D)\|_2
\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves the proposition together with the parameter-step and
conditional empirical-utility consequences.
\end{proof}

The control conditions form a guarantee hierarchy. L0,
\(\omega(D)\to0\), gives eventual inward Gaussian radial drift for each
fixed finite \(\rho\); L1, \(\omega(D)\sqrt D\to0\), removes the
absolute negative far-field force; L2, \(\omega(D)D=O(1)\), bounds the
Gaussian reuse loop gain; and L3,
\(D^k\omega(D)\to0\) for every finite \(k\), removes all finite-order
tails. Global scaling fails L0, reciprocal-quadratic tapering is critical at
L2, and exponential tapering satisfies L0--L3. 

This hierarchy also organizes the experimental controls. Positive-only and
global scaling ignore remoteness; reciprocal tapers have polynomial tails, and the quadratic member meets the matching
order; DRPO uses the exponential member that satisfies
L0--L3. These controls share the actor form in
Equation~\eqref{eq:drpo_actor_field} and differ only in their negative-branch
coefficient \citep{peng2019advantage,nair2020awac,kostrikov2021offline,
grigsby2021closer,arnal2025asymmetric,leroux2025tapered}.



\subsection{Categorical One-vs-Rest Dynamics}
\label{app:categorical_self_limiting}

\begin{theorem}[Categorical self-limiting suppression]
\label{thm:categorical_self_limiting}
For a repeatedly suppressed action \(i\), define
\(y_i=z_i-\log\sum_{j\ne i}e^{z_j}\), so \(\pi_i=\sigma(y_i)\).
Under an uncontrolled negative atom of mass \(q>0\),
\[
\dot y_i=-q(1-\pi_i)c_K(t),
\qquad \frac K{K-1}\le c_K(t)\le2.
\]
Hence \(y_i\) eventually decreases linearly and \(\pi_i\) exponentially.
With \(\omega_i=\min\{1,(e^{\tau_i}\pi_i)^\beta\}\),
\(\beta=\lambda/c\), the far branch instead satisfies
\[
y_i(t)=-\beta^{-1}\log t+O(1),
\qquad \pi_i(t)=\Theta(t^{-1/\beta}).
\]
Thus tapering changes persistent suppression from exponential to
polynomial probability decay; it does not freeze the action probability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:categorical_self_limiting}]
For a single negative atom \(i\), the direct-logit field is
\[
\dot z=-q(e_i-\pi),
\qquad
\dot z_i=-q(1-\pi_i),
\qquad
\dot z_j=q\pi_j\quad(j\ne i).
\]
Define the normalized off-target probabilities
\(\bar\pi_j=\pi_j/(1-\pi_i)\). Differentiating
\(y_i=z_i-\log\sum_{j\ne i}e^{z_j}\) gives
\begin{align}
\dot y_i
&=-q(1-\pi_i)
-q\frac{\sum_{j\ne i}\pi_j^2}{1-\pi_i} \\
&=-q(1-\pi_i)c_K(t),
\qquad
c_K(t)=1+\sum_{j\ne i}\bar\pi_j^2.
\label{eq:app_categorical_exact_ode}
\end{align}
Because \(\bar\pi\) is a distribution on \(K-1\) actions,
\[
\frac1{K-1}\le\sum_{j\ne i}\bar\pi_j^2\le1,
\]
which proves the coefficient envelope without a concentration assumption.

We next justify entry into the far half-line before using its bounds.
The log-odds is strictly decreasing. Until \(\pi_i<1/2\), monotonicity
gives \(1-\pi_i(t)\ge1-\pi_i(0)>0\), so
Equation~\eqref{eq:app_categorical_exact_ode} has a strictly negative
upper bound. The trajectory therefore crosses \(\pi_i=1/2\) in finite
time. Thereafter \(1-\pi_i\ge1/2\), so \(y_i\to-\infty\) at a linear
rate and \(\pi_i=\sigma(y_i)\) decays exponentially.

For the generic coefficient, suppose \(j^*\ne i\) is the unique largest
off-target logit initially. For every \(k\ne i,j^*\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(z_{j^*}-z_k)=q(\pi_{j^*}-\pi_k)>0.
\]
The initial ordering is therefore preserved. If a gap stayed bounded,
then, once \(\pi_i\to0\), maximality of \(j^*\) and the positive initial
gap would give a positive lower bound on
\(\pi_{j^*}-\pi_k\), a contradiction. Hence all such gaps diverge,
off-target mass concentrates on \(j^*\), and \(c_K(t)\to2\). Under
complete off-target symmetry, symmetry is preserved and
\(\bar\pi_j=1/(K-1)\), giving
\(c_K(t)\equiv K/(K-1)\).

Now apply the taper
\(\omega_i=\min\{1,(e^{\tau_i}\pi_i)^\beta\}\). Since the tapered
log-odds is strictly decreasing with a rate bounded away from zero on every
bounded log-odds interval, it reaches the far branch
\(\pi_i<\min\{1/2,e^{-\tau_i}\}\) in finite time. Thereafter let
\(U=e^{-\beta y_i}\). Since
\(\pi_i e^{-y_i}=1-\pi_i\),
\begin{align}
\dot U
&=-\beta e^{-\beta y_i}\dot y_i \\
&=\beta q e^{\beta\tau_i}
c_K(t)(1-\pi_i)^{\beta+1}.
\label{eq:app_categorical_tapered_u}
\end{align}
Thereafter, the right-hand side is bounded above
and below by positive constants. Thus \(U=\Theta(t)\), which is
equivalent to
\[
y_i(t)=-\beta^{-1}\log t+O(1),
\qquad
\pi_i(t)=\Theta(t^{-1/\beta}).
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Categorical Restoring-Force Balance}
\label{app:categorical_restoring_balance}

Write the one-dimensional restored dynamics as
\[
\dot y=F(y)=E(y)-S_{\mathrm{tap}}(y),
\]
where \(S_{\mathrm{tap}}>0\) is downward suppression and \(E>0\) is
upward restoration.

\begin{proposition}[Finite restoring crossing]
\label{prop:categorical_restoring_crossing}
Assume \(E\) and \(S_{\mathrm{tap}}\) are continuous and the zeros of
\(F=E-S_{\mathrm{tap}}\) in \([y_L,y_R]\) are isolated. If
\(F(y_L)>0\) and \(F(y_R)<0\), then the interval contains a zero at
which the phase line has a positive-to-negative crossing. Such a
crossing is locally asymptotically stable; if it is hyperbolic, its
condition is
\[
F'(y^*)<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[S_{\mathrm{tap}}-E]'(y^*)>0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The intermediate value theorem gives at least one zero. Because the
zeros of \(F\) are isolated on a compact interval, only finitely many occur; as
the endpoint signs differ, at least one has positive sign immediately
to its left and negative sign immediately to its right. The flow then
points toward that zero from both sides, proving phase-line stability. At a
hyperbolic crossing this sign change is equivalent to \(F'(y^*)<0\),
and the displayed derivative identity follows from \(F=E-S_{\mathrm{tap}}\).
\end{proof}

In the binary or symmetric one-vs-rest tail, the tapered suppression
has order \(e^{\beta y}\), whereas entropy restoration has order
\(|y|e^y\) as \(y\to-\infty\). Hence \(\beta\ge1\) supplies the
required far-tail ordering (with the \(|y|\) factor breaking the tie at
\(\beta=1\)), but it does not by itself verify the finite phase-line
conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:categorical_restoring_crossing}.

\section{Finite-Measure I-Divergence Reweighting Interpretation}
\label{app:distributional_reweighting}
\label{app:finite_measure_variational}

DRPO's exponential attenuation admits an optimistic finite-measure analogue
of divergence-ball DRO under generalized I-divergence
\citep{csiszar1975divergence}. Related optimistic distributional objectives
appear in likelihood approximation and policy optimization
\citep{nguyen2019optimistic,song2020optimistic}. Unlike
normalized-distribution ambiguity sets, the finite negative-update measure
need not preserve total mass, so reweighting may attenuate total
negative-update mass. Let
\(d\xi^-(z)=\widehat A^-(z)d\nu(z)\) and
\(h(z)=[D(z)-\tau(z)]_+\). Consider
\begin{equation}
\min_{0\le w\le1}\int wh\,d\xi^-
\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
\int(w\log w-w+1)d\xi^-\le\delta.
\label{eq:finite_measure_variational}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-measure exponential reweighting]
\label{prop:finite_measure_variational}
Let \(\delta_0=\xi^-(\{h>0\})\). For
\(0<\delta<\delta_0\), the constraint in
Equation~\eqref{eq:finite_measure_variational} is active and has a unique
finite \(\kappa>0\) with
\(w_\kappa(z)=e^{-h(z)/\kappa}\). At \(\delta=\delta_0\), the endpoint is
\(w_0=\mathbf1\{h=0\}\); for \(\delta>\delta_0\), zero objective is
already attainable and no finite one-to-one \(\kappa\)--\(\delta\)
relation remains. The later endpoint \(\delta\ge|\xi^-|\) merely makes
\(w\equiv0\) feasible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:finite_measure_variational}]
Use the convention
\(\phi(0)=1\) for
\(\phi(w)=w\log w-w+1\). For an active constraint, the Lagrangian with
multiplier \(\kappa>0\) is
\[
\mathcal L(w,\kappa)
=\int\{wh+\kappa\phi(w)\}\,d\xi^- -\kappa\delta.
\]
Pointwise stationarity on \(0<w\le1\) gives
\(h+\kappa\log w=0\), hence
\[
w_\kappa(z)=e^{-h(z)/\kappa}.
\]
For \(h=0\), this equals one; for \(h>0\), it lies strictly between
zero and one. Define
\(\mathcal D(\kappa)=\int\phi(w_\kappa)d\xi^-\). Dominated convergence gives
\[
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\mathcal D(\kappa)=0,
\qquad
\lim_{\kappa\downarrow0}\mathcal D(\kappa)
=\xi^-(\{h>0\})=\delta_0.
\]
On a set of positive \(h\), increasing \(\kappa\) strictly increases
\(w_\kappa\) toward one and strictly decreases \(\phi(w_\kappa)\).
Thus \(\mathcal D(\kappa)\) is continuous and strictly decreasing from
\(\delta_0\) to zero, proving existence and uniqueness for
\(0<\delta<\delta_0\).

At \(\delta=\delta_0\), the limit is
\(w_0=\mathbf1\{h=0\}\), which has zero objective and divergence
\(\delta_0\). For \(\delta>\delta_0\), the same zero-objective point is
strictly feasible, so the constraint need not be active and there is no
finite one-to-one multiplier relation. Finally,
\(\int\phi(0)d\xi^-=|\xi^-|\), so \(w\equiv0\) becomes feasible only at the later
endpoint \(\delta\ge|\xi^-|\).
\end{proof}



\section{Where Off-Policy Controls Intervene}
\label{app:topr_relation}

A weighted actor direction has the schematic form
\[
\mathbf g(\theta)
\propto
\mathbb E_{z\sim\nu}
\left[
    w_\theta(z)\,
    \widehat A(z)\,
    \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)
\right].
\]
We use an operational distinction: data controls alter the base measure
\(\nu\); signal controls transform the signed feedback or apply
sign-branch-wide scaling; sample-influence controls hold the sampled data
and signed feedback fixed while conditioning per-sample influence on
policy-dependent quantities. Policy anchoring instead uses dataset actions
as fitting targets or a reference policy to define policy optimization.

The taxonomy classifies intervention points rather than algorithms; a method
may instantiate more than one mechanism.

\paragraph{Sample influence.}
Canonical TOPR attenuates negative feedback using the current-to-behavior
clipped ratio
\[
w^-_{\mathrm{TOPR}}(s,a)
=
\min\left\{
    \frac{\pi_\theta(a\mid s)}{\mu(a\mid s)},1
\right\},
\]
so its control coordinate is behavior-relative mismatch
\citep{leroux2025tapered}. DRPO instead conditions negative-sample influence
on learner-relative remoteness under the current policy, with a designed
near--far boundary and decay law. Both intervene at the sample-influence
level, but they condition influence on different policy-dependent
coordinates.

\paragraph{Policy anchoring.}
Policy-anchoring mechanisms use dataset actions as fitting targets or a
reference policy to define policy optimization. TD3+BC adds
behavior-cloning pressure to the actor objective
\citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}. AGRO uses a
response-consistency-based objective derived from KL-regularized policy
optimization to learn from on- and off-policy data
\citep{tang2026agro}. DPO defines preference optimization through
reference-relative log-probability ratios
\citep{rafailov2023direct}.

\subsection{D4RL Locomotion Gradient-Diagnostic Protocol}
\label{app:d4rl_gradient_diagnostic}

\paragraph{Critic fitting and frozen advantages.}
For each of the nine D4RL locomotion task--dataset combinations, we train one
value critic, then compute, standardize, and freeze the transition-level
learned-critic advantages. All actor seeds for a given task--dataset combination
use the same frozen critic outputs.

\paragraph{Positive-only reference actors.}
For each seed, we train a Gaussian reference actor using only transitions with
positive frozen advantage. The terminal actor checkpoint is fixed before the
gradient diagnostic and serves only to define learner-relative distance and the
full-parameter gradient measurement.

\paragraph{Matched negative-transition probe.}
The probe population contains transitions with negative frozen advantage. We
form near and far pools by standardized distance, match them by absolute
advantage, and evaluate the full actor-parameter gradient on the matched probe
pairs. The probe performs no optimizer update.

\paragraph{Aggregation.}
Every far point is normalized by its original matched near partner. Curves are
first aggregated within seed and task--dataset combination; the main panel then
assigns equal weight to the nine task--dataset combinations on their shared
distance support. The 95\% confidence interval is obtained by hierarchical
bootstrap over task--dataset combinations and seeds.

\subsection{Structured-Generation Gradient-Diagnostic Protocol}
\label{app:structured_generation_gradient_diagnostic}

\paragraph{Probe definition.}
For each structured-generation task, learner-relative remoteness is
measured by the current-policy mean completion-token surprisal,
\[
D_{\mathrm{SG}}(x,y)
=
-\frac{1}{T}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}
\log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}),
\]
where only completion tokens are included and prompt and padding
positions are excluded. Define
\[
\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y)
=
\frac{1}{T}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}
\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}),
\]
and measure
\[
G_{\mathrm{SG}}(x,y)
=
\left\|
\nabla_{\theta_{\mathrm{train}}}
\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y)
\right\|_2,
\]
where the gradient is taken only with respect to trainable policy
parameters at the fixed probe checkpoint, and no optimizer update is applied
during the probe.

\paragraph{Fixed-coefficient gradient measurement.}
All probed negative responses use the same absolute negative coefficient.
Thus, negative-signal magnitude is held fixed while learner-relative
surprisal varies. This measures how the full-parameter gradient changes with
surprisal at fixed negative-signal magnitude.

\paragraph{Within-task normalization and aggregation.}
Within each task, responses are ordered by current-policy surprisal and
partitioned into ten equal-count bins. The mean gradient norm in each bin
is normalized by the lowest-surprisal bin of the same task. The
task-level normalized curves are then aggregated with equal task weight;
the corresponding task-level curves are reported separately in
Figure~\ref{fig:app_external_gradient_panels}.
Confidence intervals are obtained by cluster bootstrap within each
structured-generation task, using that task's evaluation instance as the
resampling unit, followed by equal-weight aggregation across tasks where
applicable.

\subsection{Unified Continuous Environment C-U1}
\label{app:cu1_environment}

C-U1 is the shared controlled continuous environment used by all
continuous mechanism experiments. A context
$s\in\mathbb R^6$
is sampled from
$\mathcal N(0,I_6)$.
For each seed, we independently sample 4096 training contexts and 4096
test contexts from the same distribution. Test contexts never
participate in optimization and are used only to measure
held-out-context generalization.

\paragraph{State-dependent task geometry.}
Each context determines a two-dimensional positive-support center
$a_+(s)$ and a unit task direction $u(s)$. We define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    [a_+(s)]_1
    &=
    0.70
    \tanh
    \bigl(
        0.85s_1
        -
        0.30s_2s_3
        +
        0.20\sin(1.6s_4)
    \bigr), \\
    [a_+(s)]_2
    &=
    0.65
    \tanh
    \bigl(
        -0.50s_2
        +
        0.35\cos(1.1s_5)
        +
        0.22s_1s_6
    \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_positive_center}
\end{equation}
The direction angle is
\begin{equation}

    \varphi(s)
    &=
    1.15
    \tanh
    \bigl(
        0.75s_1
        +
        0.50s_3
        -
        0.30s_6
    \bigr) \\
    &\quad+
    0.30\sin(1.35s_2),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_direction_angle}
\end{equation}
with

    u(s)
    =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\varphi(s)\\
        \sin\varphi(s)
    \end{bmatrix},
    \qquad
    v(s)

    \begin{bmatrix}
        -u_2(s)\\
        u_1(s)

\end{equation}

    a_\star(s)
    &=
    a_+(s)+0.70u(s), \\
    a_-(s)
    &=
    a_+(s)-0.50u(s).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_task_points}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Positive and negative actions.}
Each context is associated with four positive actions and eight negative
actions. The positive actions lie on a reward contour of radius
$r_+=0.75$
centered at $a_\star(s)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    a_{\mathrm{pos},k}(s)
    =
    a_\star(s)
    +
    r_+
    \bigl(
        \cos\phi_k\,u(s)
        +
        \sin\phi_k\,v(s)
    \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_positive_actions}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \{\phi_k\}_{k=1}^{4}
    &=
    \{
        \pi-\theta_1,\,
        \pi+\theta_1,\,
        \pi-\theta_2,\,
        \pi+\theta_2
    \}, \\
    \theta_1
    &=
    0.20,
    \qquad
    \cos\theta_2
    =
    \frac{2(0.70)}{0.75}
    -
    \cos\theta_1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_positive_angles}
\end{equation}
This construction makes the four actions equal-reward while ensuring
that their centroid is exactly $a_+(s)$. It also leaves nonzero
conditional action spread, preventing deterministic maximum-likelihood
variance contraction from being confused with the far-field mechanism.

The eight negative actions lie on a second reward contour of radius
$r_-=1.20$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    a_{\mathrm{neg},j}(s)
    =
    a_\star(s)
    +
    r_-
    \bigl(
        \cos\psi_j\,u(s)
        +
        \sin\psi_j\,v(s)
    \bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_negative_actions}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^{8}
    =
    \left\{
        \pi,\,
        \frac{3\pi}{4},\,
        \frac{\pi}{2},\,
        \frac{\pi}{4},\,
        0,\,
        -\frac{\pi}{4},\,
        -\frac{\pi}{2},\,
        -\frac{3\pi}{4}
    \right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cu1_negative_angles}
\end{equation}
The first negative action equals $a_-(s)$. All eight negative actions
have the same distance to $a_\star(s)$ and therefore the same reward and
fixed advantage, while having different distances from the current
policy.

\paragraph{Reward and fixed advantages.}
The ground-truth reward is
\begin{equation}
    R(s,a)
    =
    \exp
    \left(
        -
        \frac{
            \lVert a-a_\star(s)\rVert_2^2
        }{
            2(0.75)^2
        }
    \right).
    \label{eq:cu1_reward}
\end{equation}
Advantages are computed once using the fixed baseline $0.40$:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat A(s,a)
    =
    R(s,a)-0.40,
    \label{eq:cu1_advantage}
\end{equation}
and remain frozen throughout optimization. Consequently, all four
positive actions have positive advantages, all eight negative actions
have negative advantages, and negative-action quality is exactly matched
within each context.

\paragraph{Policy parameterization.}
The policy is an isotropic state-conditioned Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
    \pi_\theta(a\mid s)
    =
    \mathcal N
    \left(
        \mu_\theta(s),
        \sigma_\theta^2(s)I_2
    \right).
    \label{eq:cu1_policy}
\end{equation}
Both $\mu_\theta(s)$ and the scalar
$\log\sigma_\theta(s)$
are produced by a shared two-layer ReLU MLP with 64 hidden units per
layer and separate output heads. The initial standard deviation is
$0.60$, and the learnable-variance experiments use no artificial
variance clamp.  The continuous mechanism results reported in the main
text use the fixed-variance branch unless explicitly labeled otherwise. A finite crossing of
$\log\sigma_\theta(s)<-12$
is recorded as a support or variance-boundary event, whereas nonfinite
parameters or outputs are reported separately as NaN/Inf numerical
failure.

Training minibatches are sampled by context, and all actions associated
with a selected context are retrieved together. Thus, the replicated
actions within one context are not treated as independent contexts.
Positive and negative objectives are averaged separately before their
relative coefficient is applied, preventing the four-to-eight action
count ratio from mechanically changing the total negative-update mass.

\paragraph{Use across continuous experiments.}
All C-U1 comparisons use the same contexts, reward geometry, fixed advantages,
and policy architecture. When near--far membership is used, it is recomputed
from the current standardized distance
\begin{equation}
    d_\theta(s,a)
    =
    \frac{
        \lVert a-\mu_\theta(s)\rVert_2
    }{
        \sigma_\theta(s)
    },
    \label{eq:cu1_standardized_distance}
\end{equation}
with a fixed near--far threshold of $5.0$.

\subsection{Controlled Categorical Environment D-U1}
\label{app:du1_environment}

D-U1 denotes the controlled categorical environment family used to
study persistent negative suppression, probability-support boundaries,
and shared-semantic generalization. Its shared-semantic instantiation uses a
fixed catalogue of 64 unordered actions, each associated with a
four-dimensional unit semantic vector. Action identifiers are randomly
permuted for every seed, so numerical action indices carry no geometric
ordering.

For each seed, we independently sample 2048 training contexts and 2048
test contexts from
$s\sim\mathcal N(0,I_6)$.
The two splits follow the same context distribution, and test contexts
never participate in optimization. Results on this split are therefore
reported as held-out-context or unseen-state generalization rather than
OOD generalization.

\paragraph{Semantic action catalogue.}
Let
$\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{K}\subset\mathbb R^4$,
with $K=64$, denote the unit reward embeddings of the categorical
actions. They are sampled once per seed and randomly assigned to action
identifiers. For each context, two fixed linear maps generate a
demonstrated semantic target and a candidate improvement direction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    t_+(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{unit}
    \!\left(
    W_+^\top s
    \right), \\
    \widetilde u(s)
    &=
    W_u^\top s
    -    \left\langle
        W_u^\top s,
        t_+(s)
    \right\rangle
    t_+(s), \\
    u(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{unit}
    \!\left(
        \widetilde u(s)
    \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:du1_semantic_basis}
\end{equation}
The hidden optimal target and the local negative target are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    t_\star(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{unit}
    \!\left(
        t_+(s)+\delta u(s)
    \right), \\
    t_-(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{unit}
    \!\left(
        t_+(s)-\delta u(s)
    \right),
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\delta=0.45.
\label{eq:du1_semantic_targets}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Reward and action roles.}
The ground-truth reward of action $i$ is determined by its semantic
alignment with the hidden target:
\begin{equation}
    R(s,i)
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \left(
        1+
        t_\star(s)^\top e_i
    \right).
    \label{eq:du1_reward}
\end{equation}
The hidden optimal action is
\begin{equation}
    i_\star(s)
    =
    \arg\max_i
    t_\star(s)^\top e_i.
    \label{eq:du1_hidden_optimum}
\end{equation}
It is excluded from all positive demonstrations.

For each context, the four positive actions are the available actions
with the largest similarity to $t_+(s)$. After excluding the hidden
optimum and positive actions, the local negative action is the action
most aligned with $t_-(s)$. The four far-negative actions are selected
by their alignment with $-t_+(s)$. Thus,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \mathcal P(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{Top4}_{i\neq i_\star(s)}
    \,
    t_+(s)^\top e_i, \\
    i_{\mathrm{local}}(s)
    &=
    \arg\max_{i\notin
        \{i_\star(s)\}\cup\mathcal P(s)}
    t_-(s)^\top e_i, \\
    \mathcal F(s)
    &=
    \operatorname{Top4}_{i\notin
        \{i_\star(s),i_{\mathrm{local}}(s)\}
        \cup\mathcal P(s)}
    \,
    \bigl[-t_+(s)^\top e_i\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:du1_action_roles}
\end{equation}
The positive and negative advantages are fixed throughout training:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat A(s,i)
    =
    \begin{cases}
        +1,
        & i\in\mathcal P(s),\\
        -1,
        & i=i_{\mathrm{local}}(s)
          \text{ or }i\in\mathcal F(s).
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:du1_fixed_advantages}
\end{equation}
Consequently, differences between local and far-negative updates cannot
be attributed to different advantage magnitudes.

\paragraph{Shared-semantic categorical policy.}
The policy uses a two-layer tanh MLP with 64 hidden units per layer to
map the context to a unit semantic direction
$q_\theta(s)\in\mathbb R^4$.
Let $\widetilde e_i$ denote the action embedding used by the policy. The
categorical logits and probabilities are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \ell_{\theta,i}(s)
    &=
    \kappa_\theta(s)
    q_\theta(s)^\top
    \widetilde e_i, \\
    \pi_\theta(i\mid s)
    &=
    \frac{
        \exp\!\left(\ell_{\theta,i}(s)\right)
    }{
        \sum_{j=1}^{K}
        \exp\!\left(\ell_{\theta,j}(s)\right)
    }.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:du1_policy}
\end{equation}
The concentration is either fixed at
$\kappa_\theta(s)=8$
or learned as
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_\theta(s)
    =
    \operatorname{softplus}
    \!\left(
        c_\theta(s)
    \right)
    +
    0.05,
    \label{eq:du1_concentration}
\end{equation}
with initial value 8 and no upper clamp.

In the semantically aligned condition,
$\widetilde e_i=e_i$.
In the shuffled control, the same policy embeddings are randomly
permuted across action identifiers while the reward embeddings and
training samples remain unchanged. This intervention preserves the
catalogue size and categorical parameterization but breaks the
correspondence between policy-side similarity and ground-truth semantic
utility.

\paragraph{Evaluation and failure events.}
We evaluate expected semantic reward, probability assigned to the hidden
optimal action, entropy, effective support, and normalized semantic
extrapolation on both training and held-out contexts. Task-performance
collapse, effective-support or concentration-boundary events, and
NaN/Inf numerical failure are reported separately. A policy can
therefore retain high reward while still approaching a support boundary;
the two outcomes are not treated as equivalent.

\paragraph{Use across categorical experiments.}
D-U1 uses fixed concentration for negative-strength sweeps, learnable
concentration for support-dynamics interventions, aligned/shuffled embeddings
for semantic-transfer controls, and a direct-softmax diagnostic for repeated
suppression.

\subsection{Structured-Generation Tasks and Dataset Construction}
\label{app:structured_generation_dataset}

The structured-generation evaluation contains nine tasks:
Countdown, Word Sorting, Spiral Matrix, Mini Sudoku, Maze, Word Ladder,
Knights \& Knaves, Graph Coloring, and WikiSQL. Countdown follows the

\citep{stojanovski2025reasoninggym}; WikiSQL follows
\citet{zhong2017seq2sql}. The versions of the task sources and generators are
fixed before data generation.

All tasks use a common prompt-indexed replay bank containing the task
identity, prompt, verifier-confirmed oracle completion, verifier outcomes,
model-independent negative completions, and task metadata.
Learner-relative quantities are excluded from bank construction:
current-policy probability, surprisal, near/far labels, and taper weights
are recomputed from the learner when required rather than stored with the
data. The replay banks remain frozen during optimization.

\section{Controlled Experimental Protocols}
\label{app:controlled_protocols}

The following protocols define the controlled source-isolation,
causal-transmission, negative-strength, and taper-comparison experiments.
C-U1 and D-U1 are defined in Appendix~\ref{app:experimental_details};
experiment-specific training and evaluation details are given with the
corresponding protocol.

\subsection{Shared Notation and Controls}
\label{app:controlled_shared}

For a negative sample
$z=(s,a)$, define its raw repulsive update as
\begin{equation}
    g_\theta^-(z)
    =
    -\widehat A^-(z)
    \nabla_\theta
    \log\pi_\theta(a\mid s),
    \qquad
    \widehat A^-(z)
    =
    \max\{-\widehat A(z),0\}.
    \label{eq:controlled_negative_update}
\end{equation}
Learner-relative remoteness is measured by
\begin{equation}
    D_\theta(z)
    =
    -\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s),
    \label{eq:controlled_remoteness}
\end{equation}
or by its equivalent standardized Gaussian component when additive
density constants are irrelevant.

Within each controlled comparison, all branches use the same contexts,
samples, fixed rewards or advantage labels, initialization, optimizer,
minibatch order, training horizon, and evaluation schedule. Only the
intervention variable changes. When multiple actions share a context,
statistical aggregation is performed at the context level rather than treating
those actions as independent contexts.


\subsection{Source-Isolation Protocol}
\label{app:source_isolation_protocol}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The source-isolation experiment asks whether policy-relative remoteness
changes negative-update magnitude when sample quality is held fixed. It
identifies the source of a per-sample difference; the causal-transmission
protocol below tests its downstream effect on training.

\paragraph{Continuous product-manifold construction.}
For each C-U1 context, we select multiple negative actions from a common
reward contour:
\begin{equation}
    R(s,a_i)
    =
    R(s,a_j),
    \qquad
    \widehat A(s,a_i)
    =
    \widehat A(s,a_j)
    <0,
    \label{eq:source_equal_quality}
\end{equation}
while ensuring that
\begin{equation}
    D_\theta(s,a_i)
    \neq
    D_\theta(s,a_j).
    \label{eq:source_different_remoteness}
\end{equation}
The reward coordinate and the policy-distance coordinate are therefore
independently controlled. This exact counterfactual comparison is the
reason for using a synthetic product-manifold construction: an external
offline dataset generally cannot provide two actions with precisely
matched task quality and independently prescribed distance from the
same current policy.

The comparison is performed both at policy initialization and after
positive-only pretraining. No negative sample used as a probe is applied
as an optimizer update during this protocol. Let \(u_\theta(s)\) denote
the policy-output coordinate used by the controlled probe, e.g.,
fixed-covariance Gaussian mean coordinates in C-U1 and direct logits in
D-U1. For every probe, we record
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{score}}(z)
&=
\left\|
\nabla_{u}\log \pi_{u}(a\mid s)
\right\|_2,\\
I_{\mathrm{sample}}(z)
&=
|\widehat A(z)|\, I_{\mathrm{score}}(z).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:source_influence_metrics}
\end{equation}
Because \(|\widehat A|\) is identical across the matched actions,
differences in \(I_{\mathrm{sample}}\) arise from policy-score geometry
rather than advantage magnitude.

For neural policies, we also measure the full parameter-gradient norm and the
aggregate gradient obtained from samples in the same remoteness range. These
full-parameter actor-gradient diagnostics assess transfer in trainable networks,
complementing the output-coordinate score measures used for controlled source
isolation.

We report near-to-far ratios of remoteness, score norm, single-sample
gradient norm, aggregate gradient norm, and gradient-direction
coherence. Ratios are first computed within each context and seed and
are then aggregated across seeds.

\paragraph{Categorical counterpart.}
D-U1 uses unordered categorical actions with identical fixed negative
labels. The comparison varies the current probability assigned to the
negative action while preserving its reward role and advantage:
\begin{equation}
    \widehat A(s,i)
    =
    \widehat A(s,j)
    <0,
    \qquad
    \pi_\theta(i\mid s)
    \neq
    \pi_\theta(j\mid s).
    \label{eq:categorical_source_match}
\end{equation}
For a direct categorical logit parameterization, the score norm is
bounded and need not grow without limit as probability decreases.
The relevant far-field effect is instead persistence: the negative
update continues to increase the selected action's logit gap even after
its probability has become small. We therefore report probability,
surprisal, logit gap, score norm, and cumulative suppression over
repeated updates. This distinguishes categorical support suppression
from the unbounded Gaussian-distance amplification measured in C-U1.


\subsection{Causal-Transmission Protocol}
\label{app:causal_transmission_protocol}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
The source-isolation protocol identifies the source of abnormal update
magnitude but not its effect on task behavior. Causal transmission is tested
through near--far interventions in C-U1 and through the categorical support
reconstruction in D-U1. The primary C-U1 causal result in the main text uses
the fixed-variance branch to test mean drift and task-performance collapse.
The learnable-variance branch provides complementary evidence on support
contraction and variance-boundary dynamics.

\paragraph{Dynamic near--far partition.}
At each intervention step, negative samples are partitioned using their
current-policy remoteness. Let
$\mathcal N_\theta$
and
$\mathcal F_\theta$
denote the prespecified near- and far-field sets. Their membership is
recomputed as the policy evolves rather than frozen at initialization.
The thresholds or quantiles are fixed before training and specified with the
corresponding environment.

\paragraph{Interventions.}
For a negative sample $z$, the following multipliers are applied to
$g_\theta^-(z)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    w_{\mathrm{uncontrolled}}(z)
    &=1,\\
    w_{\mathrm{positive}}(z)
    &=0,\\
    w_{\mathrm{near\text{-}zero}}(z)
    &=
    \mathbf 1[z\notin\mathcal N_\theta],\\
    w_{\mathrm{far\text{-}zero}}(z)
    &=
    \mathbf 1[z\notin\mathcal F_\theta].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:causal_zero_interventions}
\end{equation}

Let \(G_N\) and \(G_F\) denote the aggregate near- and far-field negative
gradients on the current minibatch, respectively, and let
\(G_{\mathrm{raw}}=G_N+G_F\).

The Far-cap intervention preserves the near-field component and scales the
far-field aggregate gradient to at most \(5\%\) of the near-field magnitude:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}
    =
    \min\left\{
        1,\,
        0.05\,
        \frac{\lVert G_N\rVert_2}
             {\lVert G_F\rVert_2}
    \right\},
    \qquad
    G_{\mathrm{far\text{-}cap}}
    =
    G_N+\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}G_F,
    \label{eq:causal_far_cap}
\end{equation}
with \(\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}=1\) when \(G_F=0\).

To distinguish selective intervention from a global rescaling of negative
updates, the global-matched control applies one scalar to the full negative
gradient so that its aggregate magnitude matches Far-cap:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\mathrm{match}}
    =
    \frac{
        \lVert G_{\mathrm{far\text{-}cap}}\rVert_2
    }{
        \lVert G_{\mathrm{raw}}\rVert_2
    },
    \qquad
    G_{\mathrm{global}}
    =
    \alpha_{\mathrm{match}}G_{\mathrm{raw}},
    \label{eq:causal_global_weight}
\end{equation}
for \(G_{\mathrm{raw}}\neq0\); when \(G_{\mathrm{raw}}=0\), the matched global
negative gradient is zero.

The Far-to-near control retains the capped far-field component and rescales
the near-field aggregate gradient to restore the uncontrolled
aggregate-gradient norm. It chooses the larger nonnegative solution \(c_N\)
of
\begin{equation}
    \left\|
        c_N G_N+\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}G_F
    \right\|_2
    =
    \left\|
        G_N+G_F
    \right\|_2,
    \label{eq:causal_far_to_near}
\end{equation}
and uses
\(
G_{\mathrm{far\to near}}
=
c_N G_N+\gamma_{\mathrm{cap}}G_F.
\)
All matching coefficients are treated as detached during the policy update.

\paragraph{Causal contrasts.}
The primary causal contrast is between removing near-field and
far-field negative updates. If Near-zero leaves the dynamics unchanged
while Far-zero or Far-cap alters them, the effect cannot be attributed
to negative feedback in general. The global-matched branch quantifies
how much of the change follows from globally rescaling negative updates
and how much requires selecting samples by remoteness.

\paragraph{Temporal and terminal measurements.}
We record policy displacement, reward, negative-gradient magnitude,
mean dynamics and, for learnable-variance runs, scale dynamics, entropy or effective support, and the first
time at which each prespecified event occurs. The analysis checks whether
far-field update magnitude rises before policy drift and whether drift precedes
task-performance loss.

Terminal outcomes are classified separately as:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{task-performance collapse}: a sustained loss of the
    prespecified task metric while optimization remains finite;
    \item \textbf{support or variance-boundary event}: categorical
    effective support or Gaussian variance crosses its prespecified
    boundary without requiring NaN or Inf;
    \item \textbf{numerical collapse}: a parameter, loss, gradient, or
    model output becomes NaN or Inf.
\end{enumerate}

For C-U1, task-performance collapse is recorded when held-out-context reward
falls below \(45\%\) of the branch-initial reference reward for three
consecutive evaluations. Boundary events and task-performance collapse are
reported separately.

\subsection{Controlled Negative-Strength Sweep}
\label{app:negative_strength_sweep}

The controlled negative-strength sweep varies only the effective
negative-repulsion strength $q/p$, where $p$ denotes the aggregate
positive-attraction budget and $q$ denotes the aggregate
negative-repulsion budget. All sweep arms share the same data, fixed
labels, initialization, optimizer, training horizon, and event
thresholds. The positive-only baseline corresponds to $q/p=0$.

Held-out-context reward evaluates unseen-state generalization within the
C-U1 controlled protocol. Reward retention is terminal held-out-context reward
normalized by the corresponding branch-initial reward. Normalized policy shift
is the displacement of the policy mean from the Positive-only target along the
hidden-optimum direction, normalized by the \(0.70\) gap between \(a_+(s)\)
and \(a_\star(s)\):
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{policy}}
=
\mathbb E_s
\left[
\frac{
\langle \mu_\theta(s)-a_+(s),\,u(s)\rangle
}{
0.70
}
\right].
\]
Task-performance collapse, support or variance-boundary events, and
NaN/Inf numerical failures are reported as separate event types.


\subsection{Controlled Taper Comparison}
\label{app:controlled_taper_protocol}

\paragraph{Controlled utility construction.}
In C-U1, the hidden optimum $a_\star(s)$ lies beyond the center of the
positive demonstrations $a_+(s)$. A local negative anchor is placed on
the opposite side of $a_+(s)$, so repulsion from this anchor points
toward $a_\star(s)$. Additional negative actions are placed on matched
reward contours at larger policy-relative distances. Increasing
negative strength therefore produces three observable regimes:
positive-only under-extrapolation, useful local extrapolation, and
far-field over-extrapolation or instability.

D-U1 implements the same logic with unordered semantic actions. The
hidden optimal target lies beyond the demonstrated positive direction,
while the local negative target lies in the opposite semantic
direction. Repelling the local negative action can therefore improve
the shared representation toward the hidden optimal action. Additional
remote negative actions create support pressure without changing their
fixed negative label. Evaluation on independently sampled contexts is
reported as held-out-context generalization.

Let
\[
x(D)
=
\left[
\frac{D-\tau}{c}
\right]_{+},
\qquad
r_x(D)=\sqrt{x(D)}.
\]
The Linear and Quadratic names refer to polynomial order in the
radialized coordinate \(r_\theta=\sqrt{x_\theta}\), rather than in the
squared-remoteness coordinate \(x_\theta\).
\paragraph{Weighting rules.}
For negative-sample remoteness $D$, the compared rules are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \omega_{\mathrm{positive}}(D)
    &=0,\\
    \omega_{\mathrm{uncontrolled}}(D)
    &=1,\\
    \omega_{\mathrm{global}}(D)
    &=\alpha,\\
    \omega_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}L}}(D)
    &=
    \frac{1}{1+\lambda r_x(D)},\\
    \omega_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}Q}}(D)
    &=
    \frac{1}{1+\lambda r_x(D)^2},\\
    \omega_{\mathrm{exp}}(D)
    &=
    \exp\{-\lambda r_x(D)^2\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:controlled_taper_rules}
\end{equation}
All weighting values are computed from detached remoteness statistics;
the policy cannot reduce its loss by differentiating through the taper.

\paragraph{Near-field-retention matching.}
Let $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ denote the fixed Positive-only reference policy
used for calibration and define the prespecified near set
\[
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\{z:d_{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}}(s,a)\leq5.0\}.
\]
For a C-U1 taper $w_c(d)$, define its mean retained near-field weight as
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{near}}(w_c)
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal N_{\mathrm{ref}}|}
    \sum_{z\in\mathcal N_{\mathrm{ref}}}
    w_c\!\left(d_{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}}(s,a)\right).
    \label{eq:near_retention}
\end{equation}
Taper parameters are calibrated on the pooled training negatives so that
\(\rho_{\mathrm{near}}=0.75\), and are then frozen for the matched comparison.
This isolates far-field taper shape after matching the average weight retained
on the same prespecified near region.

\paragraph{Aggregate-gradient matching.}
At update \(t\), let \(G^-_{m,t}\) denote method \(m\)'s aggregate negative
gradient after applying its taper rule but before the matching scalar and the
Adam update. Reciprocal-Linear supplies the reference norm
\(\lVert G^-_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}L},t}\rVert_2\). For a nonzero candidate
gradient, each matched method uses the detached scalar
\begin{equation}
    s_{m,t}
    =
    \frac{
        \lVert G^-_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}L},t}\rVert_2
    }{
        \lVert G^-_{m,t}\rVert_2
    },
    \qquad
    \widetilde G^-_{m,t}
    =
    s_{m,t}G^-_{m,t}.
    \label{eq:taper_budget}
\end{equation}
The non-selective Global control uses the unweighted negative-gradient
direction and the same target norm. Thus, the matched methods share the same
pre-Adam negative-gradient \(L_2\) norm at each update while differing in how
that gradient is distributed across near and far samples. The Adam
parameter-update norm is not the matching target.

\paragraph{Measurements.}
Each method is evaluated over the full training horizon.
We report task performance, held-out-context generalization, distance
from the hidden optimum, policy displacement, entropy or variance,
effective support, near-field retention, pre-Adam negative-gradient norm,
and all three terminal-event categories. Method comparisons use
the same seeds and paired statistics.

We compare positive-only learning, global negative scaling,

tapers. Positive-only removes both components, whereas global scaling reduces
them indiscriminately.



\subsection{Shared Objectives and Negative-Update Controls}
\label{app:shared_methods}

This section defines the protocol controls and remoteness-aware
weighting rules shared by the D4RL and structured-generation experiments.
Their task-specific remoteness signals, data construction, and optimization
protocols are described separately in
Appendices~\ref{app:d4rl_methods} and
\ref{app:structured_generation_methods}.

\paragraph{Shared signed objective.}

Let \(z\) denote an offline transition or response, let
\(A(z)\) denote its fixed signed learning signal during an actor update,
and let \(D_\theta(z)\) denote its learner-relative remoteness under the
current policy. We write

\[
A^{+}(z)=\max\{A(z),0\},
\qquad
A^{-}(z)=\max\{-A(z),0\}.
\]

The shared family of actor objectives is

\[
\mathcal{J}_{\omega}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{B}}
\left[
A^{+}(z)\log \pi_\theta(z)
 -
\omega\!\left(D_\theta(z)\right)
A^{-}(z)\log \pi_\theta(z)
\right],
\]

where positive updates retain unit weight and
\(\omega(D)\) controls only the negative branch. During each actor
update, the advantage and remoteness-derived weight are treated as
fixed coefficients; gradients are not propagated through the weighting
rule.

For the remoteness-aware methods, define the normalized excess
remoteness

\[
x_\theta(z)
=
\left[
\frac{D_\theta(z)-\tau}{c}
\right]_{+},
\]

where \(\tau\) is the near-field threshold, \(c>0\) is the task-specific
scale, and \([u]_{+}=\max\{u,0\}\). Consequently, samples inside the
near-field region \(D_\theta(z)\leq\tau\) retain their full negative
weight.

\paragraph{Positive-only.}

Positive-only removes all negative updates:

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{pos}}(D)=0.
\]

It therefore provides an extreme no-repulsion reference. In D4RL it
retains only transitions with positive actor advantages; in
structured-generation tasks it retains only verifier-confirmed positive
responses under the corresponding task signal.

\paragraph{Global-\(\alpha\).}

Global-\(\alpha\) applies the same attenuation to every negative sample:

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{global}}(D)=\alpha,
\qquad
0\leq\alpha\leq 1.
\]

This control tests whether performance improvements can be explained by
a global reduction in negative-update magnitude, without using
learner-relative remoteness.

\paragraph{Reciprocal-Linear and Reciprocal-Quadratic.}
Using the normalized excess remoteness defined above, let
\[
r_\theta(z)=\sqrt{x_\theta(z)}.
\]
For Gaussian policies, \(r_\theta\) is asymptotically proportional to
standardized distance in the far field; for sequence policies, it is a
surprisal-derived radial coordinate rather than a geometric distance in
the discrete action space.
The two reciprocal controls use the same remoteness signal and
near-field threshold as DRPO, but differ in their far-field decay:

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}L}}(D)
=
\frac{1}{1+\lambda r_\theta(z)},
\]

and

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{Rec\text{-}Q}}(D)
=
\frac{1}{1+\lambda r_\theta(z)^2}
=
\frac{1}{1+\lambda x_\theta(z)}.
\]

Reciprocal-Linear has the slowest far-field attenuation. The quadratic
variant decays more rapidly but retains a polynomial tail. These
controls isolate whether the benefit comes merely from using a
remoteness signal or specifically from the decay profile selected by
DRPO.

\paragraph{DRPO.}

DRPO uses an exponential remoteness taper:

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{DRPO}}(D)
=
\exp\left\{
-\lambda x_\theta(z)
\right\}.
\]

The weight equals one throughout the near-field region and then decays
exponentially once remoteness exceeds \(\tau\). Thus, DRPO preserves
local negative feedback while suppressing the remote negative tail more
rapidly than the reciprocal alternatives.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter selection.}

Tuned hyperparameters are selected using validation data only; fixed settings
are specified with each experimental protocol. Test-task performance is not
used for hyperparameter selection.


\subsection{D4RL Baselines and Experimental Protocol}
\label{app:d4rl_methods}

Positive-only, Global-\(\alpha\), Reciprocal-Linear,
Reciprocal-Quadratic, and DRPO follow Appendix~\ref{app:shared_methods}
and are evaluated under the same D4RL actor-training protocol, differing
only in their negative-update weighting rule. This section specifies the
D4RL-specific baselines, remoteness construction, training protocol, and
evaluation procedure.

\paragraph{Tasks and datasets.}

We evaluate HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2d on the medium,
medium-replay, and medium-expert D4RL datasets
\citep{fu2020d4rl}, producing the nine dataset cells reported in
Table~\ref{tab:d4rl_performance}. The task-level comparison and external
gradient diagnostic in Appendix~\ref{app:d4rl_gradient_diagnostic} cover the
same nine cells. The same fixed offline dataset is used by every
protocol-matched method within a task.

\paragraph{Published offline-RL baselines.}

CQL, TD3+BC, and IQL are reported using their published task-level means.
Because they were not rerun under our actor-training protocol, they are marked
by \(\dagger\) in Table~\ref{tab:d4rl_performance}.

\paragraph{Gaussian-policy remoteness.}

For an action \(a\in\mathbb{R}^{d_a}\) and current Gaussian policy with
mean \(\mu_\theta(s)\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_\theta(s)\), we use the
following standardized remoteness statistic

\[
D_{\mathrm{D4RL}}(s,a)
=
\frac{1}{d_a}
\sum_{j=1}^{d_a}
\left(
\frac{a_j-\mu_{\theta,j}(s)}
     {\sigma_{\theta,j}(s)}
\right)^2.
\]

This remoteness definition is used throughout the D4RL comparisons.
When the actor uses a transformed action distribution, this quantity is
computed in the policy's native Gaussian coordinates. The resulting
remoteness is used only to determine the negative-update coefficient;
the distance term is detached during the actor update.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.}

Because remoteness distributions differ across D4RL tasks, we use
task-specific hyperparameter grids. The grids are fixed before held-out
evaluation.

\subsection{Structured-Generation Baselines and Experimental Protocol}
\label{app:structured_generation_methods}

All structured-generation experiments use
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct~\citep{qwen2024qwen25} with LoRA adaptation and
frozen prompt-indexed replay data. Learner-relative remoteness for DRPO is
the current-policy mean completion-token surprisal and is recomputed during
training rather than stored in the replay bank. In the far-field gradient
diagnostic, verifier-incorrect completions use a unit negative coefficient
throughout. Within each task, the compared methods use the same task data and
verifier labels; their shared training and evaluation settings are specified
below.

\paragraph{AsymRE.}

Asymmetric REINFORCE defines the response-level advantage using a
tunable reward baseline \(V\):

\[
A_{\mathrm{AsymRE}}(x,y)
=
r(x,y)-V,
\]

and optimizes

\[
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{AsymRE}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{B}}
\left[
\bigl(r(x,y)-V\bigr)
\log\pi_\theta(y\mid x)
\right].
\]

For a binary verifier reward, lowering \(V\) emphasizes successful
responses, whereas increasing \(V\) strengthens suppression of failed
responses. With branch-balanced signed rewards,
\(r\in\{-1,+1\}\) gives the empirical baseline
\(\widehat V=0\). The swept offset therefore determines the
objective baseline as
\(V=\widehat V+\delta_v=\delta_v\). The baseline is selected using the validation protocol and
is held fixed within each run.

Global-\(\alpha\) and AsymRE remain separate comparisons because
Global-\(\alpha\) scales the negative branch of the common signed advantage,
whereas AsymRE constructs its signed signal from the verifier reward relative
to \(V\). Under binary rewards and simplified normalization they are closely
related, but their objectives and tuning parameters remain distinct.

\paragraph{\(\beta\)-TOPR (joint fitted reference).}

Let \(\mathcal X\) be the training-prompt set. For each
\(x\in\mathcal X\), let \(y_x^+\) be the verified oracle solution and
let \(\mathcal N_x\) be the set of unique negative completions after
canonical-completion deduplication. Define the mean completion-token log
probability

\[
\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y)
=
\frac{1}{|y|}
\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}
\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}),
\]

and the full-completion summed log probability used only for the ratio,

\[
s_\theta(x,y)
=
|y|\,\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y)
=
\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}
\log\pi_\theta(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}).
\]

For the jointly fitted reference adapter \(\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}\), define

\[
s_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y)
=
\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}
\log\mu_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_t\mid x,y_{<t}),
\]
\[
\rho_\theta(x,y)
=
\exp\!\left(s_\theta(x,y)-s_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y)\right),
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_\beta(x,y)
&=
\exp\!\left(
\beta\min\{s_\theta(x,y)-s_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y),0\}
\right) \\
&=
\min\{\rho_\theta(x,y)^\beta,1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The policy objective is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J_{\beta\text{-}\mathrm{TOPR}}(\theta)
={}&
\mathbb E_{x\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal X)}
\Bigg[
\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y_x^+) \\
&-
\frac{1}{|\mathcal N_x|}
\sum_{y\in\mathcal N_x}
\operatorname{sg}[w_\beta(x,y)]
\bar{\ell}_\theta(x,y)
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\]

with actor update

\[
g_{\beta\text{-}\mathrm{TOPR}}(\theta)
=
\nabla_\theta\mathcal J_{\beta\text{-}\mathrm{TOPR}}(\theta).
\]

This objective samples prompts uniformly, assigns unit total
coefficient to the positive branch and unit total coefficient to the
negative branch, and averages unique negatives within each prompt. It
is not a simple uniform average over all response rows. The
ratio-derived weight is detached from the policy gradient.

The reference adapter is fitted with

\[
\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ref}}
=
\mathbb E_{x\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal X)}
\left[
\frac{1}{2}\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y_x^+)
+
\frac{1}{2|\mathcal N_x|}
\sum_{y\in\mathcal N_x}
\bar{\ell}_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y)
\right].
\]

The reference adapter is updated once per policy step. Reference
outputs and ratio-derived weights are detached from the policy
gradient. The 0.5/0.5 masses belong only to the reference-fitting
objective, not to the policy objective. This variant does not use behavior-policy log probabilities; its ratio
denominator is supplied by the jointly fitted reference adapter.

\paragraph{DPO.}

Canonical DPO~\citep{rafailov2023direct} is trained with the same LoRA
parameterization as the other structured-generation methods. Its policy and
frozen reference are initialized from a task-specific SFT checkpoint trained
to predict the verifier-confirmed correct completion for each training prompt;
this difference is marked by \(^{\ddagger}\) in
Table~\ref{tab:structured_generation_performance}.
For each prompt \(x\), the verified oracle solution
\(y_x^+\) is paired with every unique verifier-incorrect completion
\(y\in\mathcal N_x\). Using the summed completion log probability
\(s_\theta\) defined above, the loss is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\theta)
={}&-
\mathbb E_{x\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal X)}
\Bigg[
\frac{1}{|\mathcal N_x|}
\sum_{y\in\mathcal N_x}
\log\sigma\Bigl(
\beta\bigl[
 s_\theta(x,y_x^+)-s_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y_x^+) \\
&\hspace{8.2em}
-s_\theta(x,y)+s_{\mathrm{ref}}(x,y)
\bigr]
\Bigr)
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\]

The loss is averaged over unique negatives within each prompt and then
uniformly across prompts. The reference remains frozen throughout the
fixed 1,200-step continuation, and \(\beta\) is selected using the
validation sweep.

\paragraph{Method-specific remoteness.}

For DRPO and the other remoteness-taper controls, the learner-relative
coordinate for a prompt \(x\) and completion
\(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)\) is the current mean completion-token surprisal

\[
D_{\mathrm{SG}}(x,y)
=
-\frac{1}{T}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}
\log
\pi_\theta
\left(
y_t\mid x,y_{<t}
\right).
\]

Only completion tokens are included; prompt tokens and padding positions
are excluded. Mean rather than summed surprisal is used so that response
length does not mechanically determine DRPO remoteness. The remoteness
score is detached before applying the shared taper functions. AsymRE
does not use remoteness and applies its signed reward directly. The
Joint Fitted-Reference \(\beta\)-TOPR variant uses the dynamic
full-completion policy/reference log-ratio defined above. Canonical DPO
instead optimizes the frozen-reference pairwise log-ratio in its own
objective; neither baseline uses DRPO's mean-token remoteness.

\paragraph{Training protocol.}

All protocol-matched methods use Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct
\citep{qwen2024qwen25} with LoRA rank 32, alpha 64, and dropout 0.05. The
Joint Fitted-Reference \(\beta\)-TOPR baseline additionally initializes a
reference LoRA from the initial policy adapter. Within each structured-
generation task, the methods share the AdamW optimizer
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}, learning-rate schedule, minibatch
construction, 1,200 optimizer updates, and evaluation schedule, with no early
stopping.

Hyperparameters are selected on the validation split,
and reserved test data are not used for selection. Pass@8 is the primary
sampled-decoding metric. The nine tasks use the same training protocol. For
coefficient-response experiments, late-window Pass@8 is the arithmetic mean of
evaluations at updates \(800,900,1000,1100,\) and \(1200\).

\paragraph{Verifier-based evaluation.}

Greedy verifier success is the fraction of held-out instances solved by
greedy decoding. Pass@$k$ is the fraction solved by at least one of
the \(k\) sampled responses. Task-specific output-validity
diagnostics are reported separately from verifier success where applicable.

Task-performance degradation, token- or sequence-support boundary events,
and NaN/Inf numerical failures are reported separately.



\paragraph{Baseline coefficient selection.}
For each structured-generation transfer task, Global-\(\alpha\) scans
\(\alpha\in\{1/128,1/64,1/32,1/16,1/8,1/4,1/2,1\}\),
AsymRE scans
\(\delta_v\in\{-1.0,-0.9,-0.75,-0.5,0.0\}\), Joint Fitted-Reference
\(\beta\)-TOPR scans \(\beta\in\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}\), and canonical DPO
scans \(\beta\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1.0\}\). For DPO, \(\beta=0\)
is the SFT-only initialization control with no subsequent DPO optimizer
updates. For each task, coefficients are selected on the validation metric.

\subsection{Structured-Generation DRPO Coefficient Response}
\label{app:structured_generation_taper_sensitivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:app_structured_generation_coefficient_response} reports the
DRPO coefficient-response curves across the structured-generation tasks.
Each panel plots late-window Pass@8 against the evaluated taper coefficient
\(\lambda\), with the corresponding Positive-only result shown as a
horizontal reference.

Across tasks, the response is task dependent rather than a monotone
``larger is better'' relationship. Countdown, Word Ladder, and
Knights \& Knaves show substantial gains over Positive-only over part of the
evaluated coefficient range, while Mini Sudoku and Maze show smaller
improvements. Spiral Matrix is already near the task ceiling. Word Sorting
and WikiSQL improve as \(\lambda\) increases but the observed sweeps do not
establish a closed interior optimum. For Graph Coloring, small
\(\lambda\) is insufficient, an intermediate range is effective, and the
benefit declines as \(\lambda\) becomes large, with high-\(\lambda\)
performance returning toward the Positive-only level. These curves characterize coefficient sensitivity and task-dependent operating
ranges over the evaluated training horizon.
